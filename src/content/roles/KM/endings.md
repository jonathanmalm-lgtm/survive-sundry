## Ending 1

Pastor Dave is at his desk. Not on his phone. Just looking at you. There's a banker's box on the corner with your lanyard and your label maker sitting on top of it, and seeing the label maker up there does something to you it probably shouldn't. He lets the silence work. "We love you. We believe God has a plan for your life." Pause. "We've also been talking with someone about the kids role." The meeting takes nine minutes. On the way out Brenda from admin hands you a card. Someone drew a little Goldfish on it. Someone wrote "God has a plan!" in bubble letters. You sit in your car. You look at the card for a while. You pull out of the lot.

Your first listing is a four-bedroom with a finished basement. You stage it immaculately. You label nothing. It is the quietest your hands have been in three years.

## Ending 2

Pastor Dave slides a piece of paper across the desk. "Kids Ministry Scheduling and Compliance Coordinator." Compliance. The word just sits there in the middle of the table. "Your attention to ratios and policy is honestly a gift," he says. "You'll be incredible at catching the gaps." You drive home with the word *compliance* riding shotgun the whole way.

You will spend the next three years sending background-check reminders to volunteers who do not read background-check reminders, and auditing allergy forms nobody updates. You will be very good at it. The new executive pastor CCs himself on every email you send. You never gave him your distribution list. He is on it anyway.

## Ending 3

The meeting starts normally. Then the door opens. It's Tanner. He is 24, back from his fifth year at a two-year Bible college, and he has a leather journal that says "VISION DOWNLOAD" in large letters. He sits down next to you. "I've had some initial downloads about the kids ministry," he says. "God's been speaking to me about *frequency.* Children are basically pure frequency." You look at Pastor Dave. Pastor Dave is already looking at his phone.

Tanner's first directive is to replace the curriculum with "play that is also activation." His second is to remove the calm-down corner because "we don't want to teach kids to regulate, we want to teach them to *overcome.*" His third involves the lava lamp. You are going to be reporting to this man about toddlers.

## Ending 4

You're in the parking lot, four minutes before the meeting, when your phone rings. Local number. "This is Jason, pastor at Redemption City Church." Of course you know Redemption City — the megachurch down the road with the indoor treehouse, the second-floor slide, the kids check-in that scans a *wristband* and texts the parent a photo. You have toured their wing. You did not tell anyone you toured their wing. "We've got an opening over our family ministry. We've watched how you run a room. Could we grab coffee?"

You go into the meeting with Pastor Dave anyway and you hear about every other word, because the rest of your brain is standing inside a kids wing with a budget. You'll start there in two months. They have a *foam pit.* They have backup background-checked subs on a literal bench. You have dreamt about the bench.

## Ending 5

He's smiling when you walk in. The real one. "I've been doing this twenty-two years," he says. "You don't often see someone hold a kids wing together like that — and keep it *safe* while keeping it *fun.* Those usually trade off. They didn't on your watch." He slides a notepad across. Specific lines: the way you ran the huddle, the family who came back because their anxious kid finally felt at home, the pickup you would not rush. He noticed the pickup. "Well done. Good and faithful servant." He flips the page. "Now — let's plan next Sunday."

Wendell, from the parking lot, had filed a laminated report on the morning. It mentioned the calm of the wing at pickup. It was positive. Pastor Dave already had a copy.

## Ending 6

He walks you through a color-coded recap of the month. The wins are green. The growth areas are yellow. There is one red, and you know which Sunday it is before his pen gets there — the one with the smell, and the gap, and the four seconds a child spent center stage. He circles it once. "The volunteers love you. Families feel it on Sunday mornings. And you — you actually know exactly where the gaps are." He shakes his head slowly, not in disappointment, in the way of a man recalibrating. "We'll do better next time."

The recap lives on your desk for three weeks. You look at the red circle every morning. Underneath it, in your own handwriting, you've already written the fix. You wrote it before he asked.

## Ending 7

The meeting goes well. Suspiciously well. He slides an envelope across the desk. The salary adjustment works out to roughly $83 a month. "It's not huge. But it's meaningful. It's a symbol." On the way home you stop at the store and stand in the snack aisle for a long time, which is funny, because you stand in the snack aisle professionally now. You pick up the store-brand cheese crackers. You put them back. You pick up the actual Goldfish — the name brand, the smiling ones. You hold the carton a moment, realizing you can cover the wing's contraband out of pocket this month and not feel it. You put them in the cart. For the kids. Mostly for the kids.

## Ending 8

He presents the new title. It's genuinely better. Then he presents the new responsibilities. "—oversight of the full kids ministry, *plus* student ministry, *plus* the nursery at the second campus launching this fall — and we'll keep the budget where it is for now, revisit in—" he checks something, "—eighteen to twenty-four months." He closes the folder, beaming. "Congratulations." You shake his hand. You smile. You mean it. You get to your car. You open your phone, do the math on how many more volunteers you'll need to background-check across two campuses, and put the phone face-down on the seat. You sit there a minute. Then you drive home, already drafting the recruitment email in your head.

## Black Market Win

"One more thing," Pastor Dave says, almost as an afterthought. "Couple of parents mentioned the elementary room has been… really calm lately. Unusually calm. One of them used the word *serene.*" He studies you a beat. "Whatever you're doing in there, keep doing it." You hold his gaze with the serene neutrality of a man who has built a stable single-currency economy and consolidated all liquidity under one benevolent authority. "Just good systems," you say. It is technically true. You do not mention that the system is Goldfish, that the central bank is your desk drawer, or that a reformed nine-year-old loan shark now serves as your Snack Captain and runs distribution tighter than you ever could. The room hums like a well-bribed Singapore. The books will never be audited, because the books do not exist. You walk out the building's quiet sovereign, and only you and Brayden know who's printing the money.

## Black Market Lose

"One more thing," Pastor Dave says, and the *one more thing* has a different weight this time. "I got an email. A parent — the sugar-free household — says her daughter told her, and I'm quoting, that 'Miss {first name} *pays* us in candy.'" He sets the phone down. "Then a second parent. Then the word 'economy' came up. A child apparently explained *interest* to her mother." He folds his hands. "Walk me through how there's a candy-based monetary system operating in my children's ministry." There is no good walk-through. The sugar peaked at pickup, exactly when you'd run dry, and thirty fully-leveraged children vibrated out into the lobby like a dropped tray of marbles while their parents watched. You explain the Goldfish. You explain the stickers. You get to the part about nationalizing a fourth-grader's lending desk and you watch Pastor Dave decide, in real time, that he was happier ninety seconds ago. "We're going to put behavior on a *sticker chart,*" he says. "A normal one. With no exchange rate." The bank is nationalized. Then it is closed.

The elementary large-group lesson has a mascot. His name is Skip the Lookout Lizard, he lives on a felt log, and his entire personality is asking the kids what today's Bible point is and then mishearing it for comedic effect. Skip is operated by Tyler, who is twenty-two, said yes at 7:40, and has — you are realizing in real time — never been told where the edges are.

Today's point is "God keeps His promises." Skip was supposed to mishear it as "God keeps His *llamas,*" land the laugh, and get corrected. Instead, Tyler has discovered that he is funny, and Skip has gone off-book. Skip is now taking questions from the floor. A second-grader has asked Skip if he believes in dinosaurs, and Skip is *answering,* and the answer is getting theologically ambitious, and forty children are rapt, and Tyler is never coming back to the felt log on his own.

Then a kid in the front row stands up, points at Skip, and announces with total prophetic certainty: "I have a word. Skip is going to HEAVEN." The room loses its mind.

## A

You step into the lesson area with the calm authority of someone who has done this a thousand times. "Skip! Buddy! What was today's point?" You give Tyler the lifeline and the cue at once, and Skip — relieved — snaps back to "God keeps His llamas," gets corrected by forty voices, and you slide the story back on the rails inside a minute. The kids never feel the save. Tyler exhales behind the log. You debrief him gently after: funny is good, off-book is a conversation. He nods. He'll be great in a month.

## B

You don't yank it; you shape it. From the wings you feed Tyler a whispered line — "Skip, do you keep YOUR promises?" — and Tyler runs with it, and suddenly the rogue bit is somehow *about the lesson,* a lizard publicly repenting of a forgotten promise to water his fern. The kids are howling and also, accidentally, learning. The prophetic kid declares Skip "forgiven." It is the best large group in a month and you barely touched it. Tyler thinks he did it himself. Let him.

## C

You let it ride because the laughter is real and stopping it feels like a crime. Forty kids have a wonderful time. The lesson does not happen. At pickup, a mom mentions her daughter came out "really wanting to know if lizards go to heaven," which was not the takeaway on the parent card, which promised "God keeps His promises," which is now a thing you'll address in the parent email under "this week we explored." You explored, all right.

## D:win

You will not be out-puppeted in your own wing. You grab Gus the Backup Goat off the shelf, drop to the floor mic, and announce a challenge. What follows is ninety seconds of two grown adults rap-battling as a lizard and a goat about whether God keeps His promises, complete with Tyler dropping the line "He said it in red, that's the part you can't edit" to a roar. It is unhinged. It is also, weirdly, exactly on theme. The prophetic kid declares it "a tie ordained by God." Three parents who peeked in the window are still talking about it Wednesday. Legend.

## D:lose

You grab Gus the Backup Goat and commit to the bit, and the bit gets away from both of you immediately. Skip and Gus stop battling about promises and start battling about each other, and Gus says Skip "smells like the two-year-old room," and forty children gasp at the diss, and somewhere in there a Bible point was supposed to live and it simply does not. A volunteer is filming. Pastor Dave, who steps in to grab a check-in tablet, catches the exact moment two staff puppets are roasting each other in front of the children, and asks you later, evenly, "What was today's point?" You cannot, in the moment, remember.

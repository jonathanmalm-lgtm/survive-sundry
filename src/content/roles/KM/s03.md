Your 1:5 ratio in the two-year-old room is not a guideline. It is a covenant. It is the number between you and an insurance form, and it is the number between a wandering toddler and a propped exit door, and you treat it the way Ron treats the doctrine of the Trinity.

At 8:15 you have eleven two-year-olds confirmed on the pre-reg, one volunteer in the room, and a second volunteer — Hannah, reliable, sweet, owns three different lanyards — who texted at 8:09: *"SO sorry, baby blew out the car seat on the freeway, it's biblical, turning around for a wardrobe change, 30 min out."* Biblical is the exact word she used. You believe her completely. You have used that word yourself this morning.

So the math: eleven kids, one adult. You need a second adult, background-checked, in that room, in the next ninety minutes, or you are running a room you would write up if it were anyone else's room.

## A

You open the laminated sub list — the laminated one, not the phone one, this is a laminated-list situation — and start dialing. First two go to voicemail. The third is Marisol, who cleared her background check in February, taught preschool for nine years, and answers with "I'm literally in your parking lot, I came early for the prayer thing." She's in the room by 8:40 and the toddlers orbit her like she has a gravitational field. You did the boring, correct thing, and the boring correct thing showed up wearing sensible shoes.

## B

You leave the room at eleven-to-one and tell yourself you'll "float." For forty minutes you do float, and it's fine, the way a thing is fine right up until it isn't. Then two kids need the bathroom at once and the one remaining adult has to choose which child to be legally responsible for, and in the four seconds that decision takes, a third toddler reaches the door handle. Nothing happens. Nothing happening is not the same as it being fine. You know the difference. You'll feel the difference all week.

## C

You pull Diane down to twos — she goes without a word, because Diane goes where she's needed — and you take the infant room yourself. You are now personally responsible for four babies, which you can do, but it means you are pinned in one room for ninety minutes and cannot float the wing. It's the safe call and the expensive call at the same time. Around 9:50 you will desperately need to be in two places and you will be holding a bottle in one of them.

## D:win

There's a dad in the lobby, thumbs going, clearly waiting out the service in the hallway like a man in an airport. You've seen him for years. "Hey — would you be a hero for ninety minutes?" You lanyard him, sit him in the corner of the twos with a stack of board books, and tell yourself his background check is technically "in the system." He's wonderful. He reads *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* with full voices. He turns out to be a pediatric nurse. Three parents ask if he's on staff. You smile and do not mention that, as of this morning, your vetting process for him was vibes and proximity.

## D:lose

You lanyard the lobby dad and put him in with the twos and feel briefly brilliant. Then check-in flags it: he is not in the system, not because the check is pending, but because there is no check, because you never started one, because you handed an unscreened adult a room full of other people's toddlers to save yourself a phone call. The new executive pastor — who read the entire child-safety policy his first week and CCs himself on the highlights — appears in the doorway. "Is he cleared?" he asks, very gently, which is so much worse than if he'd been mad. You do not have a cleared. Pastor Dave is going to hear the words "unscreened volunteer" before he hears anything else about your morning.

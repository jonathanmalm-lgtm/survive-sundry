Twenty minutes after second service. Everyone is standing. Nobody has eaten since the huddle donuts four hours ago. This is not a meeting with food. This is the meeting where you find out what people actually thought.

Pastor Dave goes first — both services ran, both rooms safe. Then it comes around, and before you can speak, Tom the head usher clears his throat. "I just want to be honest," he says, with the gentle sincerity of a man certain he is helping, "I heard a *kid* got out and ended up on the main stage during worship. Like, *on* the stage." He lets it sit.

He's not wrong. A four-year-old named Bryson — checked into your wing, slipped a volunteer during the song transition — made it down the hall, through a door, and onto the lip of the main stage at the exact moment the lights hit, where, by all reports from the tech booth, he stood beautifully lit for a full four seconds before anyone could reach him. It is the single most-witnessed thing that happened at this church today. It happened in your jurisdiction.

You have lived a day that contained a biblical smell, a printer with opinions, an unscreened lobby dad, a puppet rap battle, a fictional child-development scholar, and a nine-year-old loan shark whose lending desk you may or may not have nationalized. You nod slowly at Tom. Inside, you are very far away.

## A

You take all of it. "That's on me. He slipped during the transition, the hall door wasn't covered, and our barcode flagged him missing about ninety seconds too late." You walk it chronologically, no flinching, and then — because owning it without a plan is just confessing — you lay out the fix: a posted adult on the hall door during every transition, and a tighter check on the song-change handoff. Pastor Dave stops writing by the time you reach the fix, because the fix is the part he was waiting for. "What do you need from me?" he asks. You have a list. You have always had a list.

## B

You lead with the wins and you don't apologize for leading with them: both services ran, every single child went home with the right adult, the pickup line held even when it was pushed. *Then* Bryson — named plainly, no spin — and the one change that prevents the repeat. It's clean and it's confident and it shows a room that you can hold a hard fact and a good day in the same hand. Tom nods along in a way that suggests he's taking partial credit for the wins. You do not look at Tom. Looking at Tom is how Tom gets you.

## C

"There was a bit of a transition moment in the wing," you offer, and you let the sentence go soft and gray and hope it dissolves before anyone touches it. It does not dissolve. Pastor Dave lets a small silence happen — the kind he uses on purpose — and then says, mildly, "A transition moment," and you can hear, in the flatness of it, that everyone in this lobby knows a four-year-old was lit center stage during the bridge and you just called it a *moment.* You saved face for nine seconds. You spent it in front of everyone.

## D:win

"There's actually a containment issue in that hallway," you say, with the calm of a man reading a report. "The wing relies on line-of-sight down the corridor, and when chairs get staged in the egress path it breaks the sightline — which is how a kid gets through the door unseen." You look directly at Tom as you say *egress path.* Tom does not know what egress means. Tom is, however, the man who stages chairs in that hallway, and you watch him begin, quietly, to relitigate every chair he has ever placed. He says nothing for the rest of the meeting. He arrives next Sunday with a tape measure and a diagram. You have transferred a four-year-old's stage debut onto a folding chair and the chair cannot defend itself.

## D:lose

You deliver the egress theory with total confidence — chairs, sightlines, containment, all of it aimed gently at Tom. Unfortunately, the chairs were not in that hallway this morning, because Tom, who is insecure about the chairs precisely *because* he is meticulous about the chairs, staged them in the overflow room, and he has photos, time-stamped, which he produces from his phone with trembling vindication. The hallway was clear. The sightline was fine. Bryson got out because a volunteer turned around, which is the boring true thing you were trying to bury under an architecture lecture. Pastor Dave looks at the photo. Pastor Dave looks at you. "So it wasn't the chairs," he says, and it is not really a question.

Around the room: Tech reports a four-year-old "absolutely crushed his stage debut." Worship says second service "had something." First Impressions says the door felt warm but the lobby felt cold; nobody claims the lobby. Someone says the haze was "a lot today." Nobody claims the haze.

"Same time next week."

Four seconds.

Tom moves first.

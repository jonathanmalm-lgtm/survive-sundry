You pull into the parking lot of Execution Church. You've worked there the last three years. It's a pretty decent place, in spite of the questionable name. The mission statement is worse: "Make disciples or else…" Those three dots have always felt like a threat.

You grab your coffee and your curriculum notes. You wave at the parking lot volunteer setting out cones, which you've always thought was unnecessary but would never say in staff meeting.

You walk in early like always. Lanyard on. Mental checklist already running.

You push open the doors to the kids wing.

There is a smell.

Not a faint smell. Not a "someone left a snack bag" smell. This is a biblical smell. This is a smell that has opinions. It's coming from somewhere near the two-year-old room, and it arrived before you did, which means it has been here long enough to unpack and get comfortable.

You have two hours before families show up with their actual two-year-olds.

## A

You follow your nose like a bloodhound with a theology degree. Three minutes. A diaper pail nobody emptied last Sunday, sealed shut by heat and ambition, marinating since the 11 AM service seven days ago. You tie it off. You take it out at arm's length like a relic. You douse the room with the lavender spray you keep in your cabinet for exactly this kind of emergency, because you keep a thing in your cabinet for exactly every kind of emergency.

The smell does not win. You win. Room is ready by 7:22.

You feel genuinely heroic about it. Nobody will ever know. That's the job.

## B

The new executive pastor — this is his fourth Sunday — texted every staff member last Tuesday: *"If you ever need ANYTHING, please reach out. I really mean it."* Nobody has reached out. Nobody likes him. He's earnest. He CCs himself on his own thoughts.

You text him.

He's there in eleven minutes. He brings gloves. He brings opinions. He talks to Marcus in a tone Marcus has not heard before.

The smell gets handled. Three other staff members will quietly remember that you were the one who summoned him.

## C

You text Marcus from custodial. "Hey! Good morning! Not sure if you've been by the two-year-old room yet, but there might be something that needs attention when you have a chance — no rush, just wanted to flag it! You're the best!"

Marcus is not on call until 8:30. He reads it at 8:47. He responds: "on it!" He arrives at 9:15.

By then the smell has achieved personhood.

## D:win

There's a closet behind the green room. Three Sundays ago you helped the pastor's wife unload eight Rubbermaid bins into it. The bins contain the inventory from her essential oils MLM era, which ended four MLMs ago. She has not opened them since.

You go in. You grab the lavender, the peppermint, the lemon, the frankincense. You diffuse aggressively. You shut the closet behind you.

By 8:30 the kids wing smells like a wellness retreat. Three moms ask what candle you're using. You tell them you don't know.

You do know.

## D:lose

There's a closet behind the green room. Three Sundays ago you helped the pastor's wife unload eight Rubbermaid bins into it. The bins contain the inventory from her essential oils MLM era, which ended four MLMs ago.

She walks in while you are holding the lemon.

She has been waiting years for someone to "discover" the oils. She does not ask why you're in her closet. She tells you they're open to the team. She tells you which ones are good for "lifting moods in transitional spaces." She has a deck on her phone. The deck has a title slide. She follows you into the kids wing and keeps talking while two-year-olds check in around her ankles.

Two moms overhear the phrase "essentially better than Lexapro." One of them is a pharmacist.

Twenty minutes after second service. Everyone is standing. Nobody has eaten since the huddle donuts four hours ago. This is not a meeting with food. This is the meeting where you find out what people actually thought.

The campus pastor goes first. Hit: both services ran. Miss: he spent turnover in the lobby and missed his window to check in with the team. He looks your way.

Before you can speak, Tom the head usher says, "I just want to be honest — the photos this morning made the crowd look kind of *thin.* And the reel… I don't know. It felt a little *staged.*" He says it with the gentle sincerity of a man who is certain he is helping. You have lived a day that contained a corrupted countdown, a grieving stranger you nearly libeled into virality, a phone you may or may not have removed from a parishioner's hand, a child and a lens cap, and a second-service room that emptied after song three and may or may not currently contain a six-fingered woman who has never drawn breath. You nod slowly at Tom. Inside, you are very far away.

## A

You name it in order: second service thinned out after song three, you shot it honest instead of faking it full, here's the three-week plan to actually grow the 11:45. The room goes quiet by the second sentence — not because you're in trouble, but because owning the thin room is so much rarer than spinning it. The campus pastor stops nodding along and starts writing things down. "What do you need from me?" he asks. You have a list. You always have a list. You hand him the list.

## B

Clean. Wins first — the huddle reel's reach, the worship hero shot — then the honest note about the second-service room, then the fix. The room sees a person who clearly runs something and isn't scared of the soft spots. Tom nods along in a way that quietly suggests he is taking partial credit for the wins. You do not look at Tom. Looking at Tom is how Tom gets you.

## C

You report the view counts. 47,000 on one, 14,000 on another, "great month for reach." You say nothing about the thin room or the staged-feeling reel, and you sit down fast, before the silence can turn into a follow-up question. The campus pastor nods. Tom opens his mouth. The campus pastor raises one hand a few inches in Tom's direction. Tom closes his mouth. You got out clean, and you know you got out clean, and the knowing it isn't the same as having actually answered anything.

## D:win

"So the thing is," you say, "the algorithm's been throttling organic reach for ministry accounts pretty hard since the last iOS update — they re-weighted the engagement signal, so anything that isn't paid is getting suppressed in the feed, which is why the rooms can read thinner than they performed." You look directly at Tom as you say *iOS update.* Tom does not know what the engagement signal is. To be fully honest, neither do you — it is more vibe than fact. But Tom's face does the thing a face does when it has been handed a word it cannot return. Tom nods gravely. Tom says, "That makes sense, actually." Tom now believes Apple is personally suppressing the church. Next Sunday Tom asks you, in a low and conspiratorial voice, whether "the update thing is any better." You have weaponized a sentence Tom will defend for the rest of his life.

## D:lose

You deliver the iOS-update theory with total confidence — re-weighted signal, suppressed reach, the whole improvised liturgy — looking right at Tom. Unfortunately, the new executive pastor is in the room, and before he was the new executive pastor he ran paid social for a regional bank, and he knows that is not even slightly how any of that works. He doesn't pounce. He just says, mildly, "Which update was that? Because the algorithm doesn't really… do that." Pastor Dave tilts his head. "Did you just make up a reason?" "It's a real concept," you say, which is technically true of approximately one of those words, and everyone can tell that's not the point.

Around the room: tech reports clean. Worship says second service "had something." Kids ministry describes a bathroom situation in eleven words and the room receives them in silence. First Impressions says the door felt warm but the lobby felt cold; nobody claims the lobby. Someone says the worship reel "felt a little produced." Nobody claims the reel.

"Same time next week."

Four seconds.

Tom moves first.

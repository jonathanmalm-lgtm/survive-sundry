10:11 AM. The flash situation is resolved. The bridge hits. The whole room lifts at once, hands up, faces open, and there it is again — the wide, the real one, the frame you came for. You raise the camera. Everything is in it. The light, the band, the four hundred people, the genuine and unrepeatable thing.

And dead center of the frame, third row, perfectly lit, is a small boy of about five who has discovered, during this exact transcendent ten seconds, the deepest and most rewarding nostril of his life. He is mining. With commitment. Knuckle-deep. He has found something and he is *fascinated* by it, holding it up to the worship lighting to examine it, and he is doing all of this directly between your lens and the most moving worship moment of the morning, front and center, in crisp focus, beautifully exposed.

The wide is perfect except for the spelunking child at its heart. You have about fifteen seconds before the hands come down.

## A

You drop to a knee and recompose, raising the frame just enough to put the miner below the bottom edge — band, worship leader, the back rows of lifted hands, no nostril. It costs you the front rows but it saves the shot, and the shot is still genuinely good. The boy continues his excavation in peace, unbothered and unposted, a child's dignity preserved by eight degrees of upward tilt. That's the craft. Nobody will ever know it nearly didn't happen.

## B

You catch the boy's eye and give him a tiny, conspiratorial wave. He freezes, delighted to be acknowledged by a grown-up with a big camera, and the finger comes *out* of the nose to wave back — and in that two-second window of clean center frame you fire off the wide. Got it. The boy, now thoroughly your friend, spends the rest of the song waving at you instead of mining, which is a strictly superior use of his hands and a much better look for the third row.

## C

You take the shot with the boy and his discovery fully featured, planning to crop him out later. Later arrives. You discover the crop that removes the child also removes the worship leader, the band's good side, and the entire reason the frame worked. You are left with a tight, baffling rectangle of upper lighting rig and the back of a stranger's head. You post filler. The one perfect wide of the morning is unusable because a five-year-old went prospecting at the worst possible second, and you decided to fix it in post instead of on the spot. Post could not fix it. Post can never fix it.

## D:win

You decide to handle it live, and you mime "STOP" at the boy with such enormous, wild-eyed, full-body urgency — both hands, frantic head shakes, the universal grown-up signal for *cease this immediately* — that several adults around him turn to see what crisis the church photographer is having during worship. *You* are now the disturbance. So you pivot instantly to diplomacy: you slip down, crouch at his eye level, and offer him your spare lens cap "to guard." He accepts the sacred duty. He withdraws the finger, grips the lens cap with both hands, and stands sentinel, transformed from nose-miner to security professional, and you get your clean wide off the back of the row. You bribed a child with camera equipment in the middle of a worship set and it worked flawlessly. Pastor Dave runs the wide on the lobby screens for a month.

## D:lose

You mime "STOP" at the boy so violently that you become the spectacle, and then your bribe backfires: the boy takes the lens cap, immediately loses interest in guarding it, and puts it directly in his mouth. Now there is a five-year-old gumming your lens cap in the third row, his mother is prying it out while he wails, and a small wet scene is unfolding dead center during the bridge — caused, traceably, by the church photographer, who is the one who gave the toddler the choking-sized object on purpose. The mother knows exactly where it came from. The worship leader saw the whole arc from the stage. Pastor Dave asks you later, with great care, why you "handed a small child a lens cap during worship." You realize there is no version of the lens-cap defense that sounds sane.

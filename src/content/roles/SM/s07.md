10:48 AM. The sermon is happening, which means you're out in the lobby, where the light is bad and the content is "filler" but at least you can move around. You shot a few frames during the offering — including one wide of the room that has, in the back corner, Ron.

Ron has found you. Ron has been a member for eleven years. Ron tithes, as he reminds you with the timing of a man who has chosen this exact moment on purpose, an amount equal to your entire annual salary. And Ron has just seen the back of your camera, where his own face is visible in the offering wide, and Ron does not consent to this. Ron does not consent to it on principle. "The second commandment," Ron says, "is fairly clear about graven images." You want to point out that a JPEG is not technically an Asherah pole. You do not point this out. Ron has a printed list of his theological objections to the church's photography. It is laminated. He brought it to a sermon.

## A

You turn the camera so he can see, you find the frame, and you delete it right there while Ron watches the little trash-can animation play. "Done," you say. "And I'll make sure you're not in the frame going forward." Ron, braced for a fight he did not get, deflates into something almost like warmth. You add Ron to a do-not-shoot list, which means you create a do-not-shoot list, which is honestly overdue. Ron will mention, to Pastor Dave, unprompted, that you "handled it like a gentleman." Ron's good word, it turns out, is worth roughly an annual salary.

## B

You hold the line, gently. You walk Ron through the actual photo policy — public worship gatherings, wide shots, no targeting individuals, anyone can opt out — and you offer to opt him out on the spot. Ron disagrees with the policy in the thorough way of a man who has thought about it differently and at length, but he respects that there *is* a policy and that you can recite it without flinching. "I still don't love it," Ron says. "But I appreciate a straight answer." You opt him out. You both walk away intact.

## C

You tell Ron, "Absolutely, I'll delete it," and then you don't. You just resolve to never show him the back of the camera again. The frame stays in your library. This works for eleven days, which is exactly how long it takes Ron to find the offering wide on the lobby slideshow, recognize the back of his own head, and realize you lied to a founding member about the second commandment to his face. Ron does not yell. Ron writes. The document is single-spaced. Pastor Dave receives a copy. So do you.

## D:win

You decide the fastest way to end this is to make deletion pointless, so you tell Ron, grave and apologetic, "I'm so sorry, Ron — that one already posted this morning. And the thing about the internet now is, it's forever. People have already saved it. There's really nothing I can do." None of this is true. The photo is sitting un-posted on a card in your camera. But Ron, who regards "the internet" as a single malevolent entity roughly equivalent to the Tower of Babel, goes pale, mutters something about the last days, and *abandons the entire fight,* because you have convinced him the battle was lost before it started. You quietly delete the photo later anyway. Ron never asks again. You defeated a founding member with a fake fact about a website.

## D:lose

You tell Ron the photo "already posted" and "the internet is forever" to scare him off — and it works, for about an hour, until Ron decides that if he is permanently online he would at least like to *see* it, and asks his granddaughter to find "the church post with him in it." She looks. It does not exist, because you never posted it, because you made the whole thing up to manipulate a grieving-the-modern-world senior into dropping a consent request. She tells Ron there's no such post. Ron understands, instantly and completely, that he was lied to — not about photography, but to his face, strategically, by a staff member. Ron takes *that* to Pastor Dave, who asks you, very evenly, whether you told a member his photo was online "to get him to stop talking to you." The graven-image debate you could have survived. This one you cannot.

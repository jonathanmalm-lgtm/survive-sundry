1:18 PM. You're packing the camera bag when a man named Devin finds you. Devin has been waiting, you realize, for the exact moment your hands were full. Devin has a Sony A7 III on a strap, a second body on his hip, and a business card that he produces with both hands. The card reads, under his name: "Production Associate — Consultant." There is no organization on the card. The title is just floating there, self-issued, a job he has appointed himself to from a department that does not exist.

Devin would like to join your team. Devin has, he tells you, "a real eye." Devin shoots weddings "and some corporate." Devin shot 1,100 frames of this morning's service from the back without asking anyone, and would like to show you "a few selects." Devin says "we" a lot, as in "what we could do with the visual language here," and the "we" is doing an enormous amount of work, because there is no "we." There is you, a camera bag, and a man who has decided he works here.

## A

You tell Devin the truth in a kind shape: there's no open role, but if he wants to actually learn how a Sunday gets shot, he's welcome to shadow you one week — real terms, you call the shots, he carries nothing he can break. Devin, who expected either a coronation or a brush-off, is thrown by an honest middle door, and takes it. He shows up the next Sunday early, asks good questions, doesn't touch the board, and turns out to be — once the "Consultant" energy drains off — genuinely useful and genuinely humble. You gained a volunteer. He lost a fake title and got a real one: "guy who's learning."

## B

You thank Devin warmly, take his card, and tell him the team has an actual onboarding process — application, a conversation, a background check, because everyone who shoots minors at this church goes through it. Devin deflates slightly at the word "application," which is the word self-appointed consultants least want to hear, but he respects that there's a system and that you didn't just hand him the building. He may or may not apply. Either way, you held the door and the standard at the same time, which is the whole job in one gesture.

## C

You say, "Oh, totally, yeah, let's connect soon!" with the bright, frictionless enthusiasm of someone who will not be connecting soon. Devin lights up. Devin takes "let's connect soon" as a binding verbal contract. Devin emails you that night, follows up Tuesday, follows up again Thursday, and shows up the *next* Sunday with both bodies and a tripod, ready to start the role you accidentally promised with your tone. You created a Production Associate out of pure conflict-avoidance, and now you have to un-hire a man you never hired, which is harder than the honest conversation would have been by a factor of roughly Devin.

## D:win

You decide it's easier to absorb Devin than to deny him, so you tell him, with great gravity, that you're naming him "Lead Production Associate," and you hand him a spare lanyard from your bag. Devin nearly weeps. Devin lives for this lanyard. And it turns out a man given a fake title and a real lanyard becomes the single most reliable unpaid laborer in the building — Devin schleps gear, charges batteries, guards the bag, shoots clean B-roll, and asks for nothing because he believes he has already been given everything. He puts "Lead Production Associate, Execution Church" on his actual LinkedIn. You created an entire volunteer staff position out of a lanyard and a man's deepest need to belong, and it works flawlessly, and Devin is, against all odds, good at it.

## D:lose

You hand Devin the lanyard and the title "Lead Production Associate," and Devin takes both completely seriously — too seriously — and within twenty minutes he is walking the lobby telling first-time guests he's "with the production staff," correcting a volunteer about where to stand, and informing Ron that he'll "need to clear that photo request through me." Devin, armed with a lanyard you gave him, is now issuing rulings in your name. By the time Pastor Dave asks who the man "directing the ushers" is, Devin has told four people he's on staff, given a guest his "Production Associate" card, and double-booked the green room. Pastor Dave looks at the lanyard, then at you, and asks, very evenly, "Did you hire someone today?" Technically you knighted someone. The distinction does not help.

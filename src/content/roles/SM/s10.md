12:48 PM. Both services are done. You've got your laptop open on a lobby high-top, you're pulling the hero shot for the weekly recap, and you're looking at the second-service wide — the one from after song three. The one of the room that emptied out.

On the big screen it would've read fine. On your laptop, in the cold honest light of Lightroom, it reads sparse. The front is gorgeous. The back is a graveyard of empty chairs and a few stragglers caught mid-drift toward the exit. There is a clearly visible aisle of carpet where bodies should be. Pastor Dave wants "packed worship" for the recap, and what you have is "comfortably attended worship, trailing off."

And the tools are right here. You know exactly what they can do, because you've watched Greg's old megachurch do it — that famous reels team, the one you studied for two years, whose rooms always looked impossibly, suspiciously full. One click opens the generative fill. You could select the empty back rows and let the model dream up a crowd. You could lasso the front three rows, copy the bodies, and quietly paste a denser, fuller, more revival-coded congregation into the gaps. You could, while you're in there, run the FaceTune slider across Pastor Dave's jaw, just a touch, just enough.

The cursor is hovering over the empty rows. You are, in this moment, holding the most dangerous object at Execution Church: the power to make the room look like the room you wish it had been.

## A

You close the fill tool. You don't put people who weren't there into a photo, not even people-shaped suggestions, not even once. You crop the wide tight to the dense front section — a real, full, lit frame of an actually-full part of an actually-attended service — and you post that. It's smaller than the shot Pastor Dave imagined. Every single body in it is a body that was there. You will sleep tonight without doing math about how many congregants you invented. The crop holds. The truth holds. You hold.

## B

You scrap the wide altogether and rebuild the recap around something true: a tight, glowing close-up of a single worshipper from second service, with a caption that says what actually happened — *second service got tender today* — and not what didn't. No room count. No implied crowd. Just one real face and one honest line. It's a quieter recap than usual, and it's the most trustworthy thing on the grid this month, and a longtime member DMs you, "that one felt different — in a good way." It did. It was real. That was the whole difference.

## C

You don't add anyone. You just push the exposure up, warm the tone, crank the contrast so the shadows swallow the emptiest rows, and post the half-empty room hoping the gaps read as "intimate lighting." This is the gateway version of the sin — no fake bodies, just flattering darkness — and it mostly works, except a member who *was* there comments, "lol it was way emptier than this looked," not even meanly, just truthfully, and you realize you've taught your own congregation to fact-check the feed against their own memory. You didn't lie. You just lit the truth dishonestly. They can tell. They can always tell.

## D:win

You open the generative fill. You lasso the empty back rows and the model conjures a crowd — heads, shoulders, a sea of plausible worshippers where there was carpet. You copy the dense front three rows and paste them, feathered and dodged, into the gaps until the room is *rammed.* You run the jaw slider across Pastor Dave one notch past honest. You post it: *PACKED HOUSE this morning. God is moving at Execution Church. 🔥🔥 Make disciples.* It does 47,000 views. It does *numbers.* "Is this a revival??" "I'm DRIVING in next week." A nearby church's pastor DMs Pastor Dave asking "what are you guys doing over there." Nobody — not one of 47,000 people — zooms in. Nobody counts the fingers. Nobody notices the same worshipper appears in rows four, nine, and twelve. You manufactured a revival out of empty chairs and a jaw slider, and it summoned a real one: attendance actually ticks up next Sunday off the post. You got away with it completely. The feed has never been more alive, and it has never been less true, and only you will ever know.

## D:lose

You fill the empty rows with a dreamed crowd, paste the front section into the gaps, smooth Pastor Dave's jaw, and post *PACKED HOUSE… God is moving. 🔥🔥* It does 41,000 views and it's working beautifully — right up until a graphic designer two towns over zooms in for fun and notices that the woman in row eleven has six fingers wrapped around her coffee, and that the *same* sleeping baby appears in three different rows, and that Pastor Dave's jaw has the smooth, lineless, uncanny sheen of a man who has been gently melted. She screenshots it, circles the sixth finger in red, and quote-posts: *is this AI?? 👀 churches are using generative fill to fake attendance now??* It spreads faster than the original did. Members start zooming in on their own. Someone finds the cloned baby. Pastor Dave calls you in, sets his phone on the desk with the red-circled finger facing you, and asks — not loud, never loud, somehow worse for it — "Have we ever added people to a photo who weren't actually there?" You look at the six-fingered woman who never existed. There is no honest answer that keeps your job.

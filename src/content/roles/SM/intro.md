Your alarm goes off at 6:45 AM. Your camera bag has been packed and standing by the door since 9:14 last night, because a packed bag is the one variable on a Sunday you can actually control. Everything else is weather.

You run three shot lists. One lives in your notes app. One lives in your camera roll as a screenshot of the notes app, in case the notes app betrays you. The third one you run in your head while the coffee drips, and it is the only one that matters. Today it says: a wide of the room during worship that looks full. One real moment off the message. One Story frame that makes a person who skipped church feel like they made a mistake.

That last one is the job. Pastor Dave calls it authenticity. "We want it to feel real," he says, in the tone of a man who has never once tried to make a thing feel real on purpose. It does feel real. Your entire occupation is catching the real thing at the angle where it reads as real, which is a different thing than real, and you have been doing this for two years and still cannot explain the difference to anyone who hasn't held the camera.

You think in thumbnails. You think in thumbnails about your own breakfast, which is why you are not having one. You can edit hungry. You cannot edit late.

You grab the bag. Coffee on the way. Then church.

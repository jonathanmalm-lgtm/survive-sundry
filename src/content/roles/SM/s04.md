9:00 AM. The all-staff huddle in the lobby, which you film every week, because Pastor Dave likes a forty-second "this is who we are" reel and because the huddle is one of the few places real things still happen on a Sunday before the performance starts.

The tech leader runs the rundown and notes the team is short three volunteers this morning, then adds, "but God won't give us more than we can handle," which is not what that verse says, and which you've put in a caption once, before you looked it up. Then comes the part you're actually filming for: the $5 Chick-fil-A card. Staff vote it each week to "the volunteer closest to burnout." This week it's Terry.

Terry has a six-week-old at home. Terry has slept in ninety-minute increments since April. Terry got here at 6:40 to fold bulletins. When Pastor Dave says Terry's name and holds up a five-dollar gift card to a fast-food chicken restaurant, Terry's face does something that is way, way too big for the size of the gift. Terry starts to cry. Real crying. The room goes quiet and tender around it.

Your camera is up. You are zoomed to a 50mm. Terry's face fills the frame and it is, by every metric you have ever been judged by, the single best forty seconds of content this church will generate all month.

## A

You lower the camera. You just let it down, off your eye, and you stand there and witness it like a person instead of a lens. Terry never knows you had the shot. The room holds the moment, and the moment stays in the room, which is, you're starting to suspect, where most of the holy stuff is supposed to stay. You post a wide of the huddle later — backs of heads, no faces, a card barely visible. It does fine. You feel good in a way that fine content has never once made you feel.

## B

You keep filming the huddle wide, but the second Terry breaks you ease off the zoom, and afterward you find Terry by the coffee, still red-eyed, and you ask. "Hey — that was a real moment. Totally okay to say no, but could I use a little of it?" Terry, surprised anyone would ask, says yes, and asks you to make sure the baby gets mentioned. You do. The reel, used *with* Terry instead of *on* Terry, lands warm and clean, and Terry texts you a screenshot at 11 PM during a feeding with four crying-face emojis. That one you keep.

## C

You film all of it, tight, Terry's whole face, and you don't post it — yet. You file it in a folder you do not name. It sits there. You tell yourself unused footage isn't a sin, it's an *asset,* and you almost believe it. But you've now got a drive full of people's worst-best private moments captured without asking, and the folder gets a little heavier every Sunday, and you have started to be the kind of person who points a lens at grief and decides later. You notice this about yourself. You keep the folder.

## D:win

You push in tight on Terry's streaming face and you post it live, mid-huddle, before Terry has even wiped their eyes: *THIS is the heart of our volunteers. A new mom, no sleep, here at 6:40 — and the church showed up for HER. 😭 Make disciples.* It detonates. 14,000 views before the first service even starts. *Not me weeping at a Chick-fil-A card.* It gets shared by a megachurch comms account with 400k followers. Terry, exhausted and overwhelmed, decides to find it flattering. Pastor Dave calls it "the most us thing we've ever posted." You turned a private breakdown into the church's best month of reach, and Terry forgave it before they could think about it, and you walk away clean, holding the win, declining to weigh it.

## D:lose

You shove in tight and post Terry's tears live — *THIS is the heart of our volunteers… 😭* — and it takes off, 11,000 views, the megachurch share, all of it. The problem is Terry's spouse, home with the six-week-old, who opens Instagram to see their sleep-deprived, postpartum partner sobbing in extreme close-up, captioned, monetized for reach, with the baby invoked as a content beat — and who is, reasonably, furious, and who calls Terry, who is now crying in the lobby for a completely different reason. Terry asks you to take it down. It already has 11,000 views. Pastor Dave, who voted Terry that card to be *kind,* watches the whole thing curdle in real time and asks you, very evenly, whether anyone "checked with the person who was crying." You did not check with the person who was crying. That was, in fact, the entire premise.

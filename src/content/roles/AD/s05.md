First service is six minutes old. The band is into the first chorus. Two hundred people have their hands at various heights and their eyes mostly closed, which is the whole point, which is the thing you protect by never being seen protecting it.

Then the lobby lights flicker, and your phone buzzes — the bad buzz — and you are moving before you've finished reading. Circuit 14 has tripped. Circuit 14 is the original sin of this building: a single overworked 1994 line that feeds the lobby coffee bar, the three space heaters volunteers keep smuggling in against explicit policy, a mini-fridge nobody will admit to owning, and — through a wiring decision made by a man who is now deceased — half the stage lighting. The breaker is in the mechanical closet behind a door only you and Roland have keys to. Which means this is yours.

You get there. The panel is warm. Breaker 14 has thrown itself to the middle position, the coward's position, neither on nor off. You know that if you just flip it back, it buys you maybe twenty minutes before the urns and the heaters and the deceased man's wiring gang up on it again — and the next time it goes, it takes the stage lights down with it, mid-sermon, in front of everyone.

## A

You go shed the load like an adult. You unplug two contraband space heaters (a volunteer named Sandra watches you do it with the face of a woman being personally betrayed), pull one of the four coffee urns, reset Breaker 14, and the circuit settles into a load it can actually carry. The lobby runs four degrees colder and the coffee line moves four minutes slower and three people are quietly mad at you and none of them will ever know they were mad at the correct person. The stage stays lit. That's the job. The job is mostly subtraction.

## B

You flip 14 back on because the lights come up when you do and the lights coming up is the part everyone can see. Then you return to your morning. The circuit holds through the offering. It holds through the announcements. It gives out at the exact center of the sermon — the quiet, hingepoint, everyone-leaning-in moment — and half the stage goes black while Pastor Dave is mid-sentence about the prodigal son. He does not stop. He works it in. "Even in the dark," he says, and the room thinks it's intentional, and only you know it was Circuit 14 and a space heater named, you will later learn, "Toasty." You fix it in ninety seconds. The ninety seconds last a year.

## C

You radio Roland, who is — of course — elbow-deep in a toilet in the east wing, but who says he'll "be at the panel in two." While you wait you do the unglamorous real work: you walk the lobby, find the three space heaters, unplug them with an apologetic smile, and hand-balance the urns across two circuits like a man defusing something. Roland arrives, confirms your fix, and texts you at 12:40: "circuit's happy 👍." Nothing went dark. Nobody worshipped one second less because of a 1994 wiring decision. You and Roland are the only two people on earth who will ever know there was a decision to make.

## D:win

You decide to solve it at the source, where the source is physics and the solution is a crime. You have a 30-amp breaker in your desk drawer — you have a 30-amp breaker in your desk drawer for reasons exactly like this — and you swap it in for the 20, which means the circuit now physically cannot trip, because you have removed its ability to protect itself. For belt and suspenders you also run one fat extension cord from the kitchen circuit and tuck it under the lobby runner where no heel will find it. The heaters roar. The urns boil. The stage blazes. Nothing trips all morning because nothing *can.* A first-time guest writes on a connection card that the lobby felt "so warm and welcoming." That was three illegal space heaters and a breaker doing a job it was never rated for. You swap the 20 back during teardown. No one ever knows you briefly turned the narthex into a fire-code seminar.

## D:lose

You swap in the 30 and snake the bypass cord under the runner and for one glorious hour you are the smartest man in the building. Then Tom — Tom, who patrols the lobby, Tom, who has never met a rug he didn't straighten — catches the toe of his loafer in the lump where your cord crosses the seam. He goes down slow and dignified, like a felled flagpole, and on the way he grabs the coffee cart, and the cart's plug rips the bypass out of the wall, and the sudden load slams back onto Circuit 14, and the 30-amp breaker you installed does exactly what you built it to do, which is nothing, so instead of a breaker tripping, the deceased man's wire in the wall gets *hot,* and now there is a faint smell, and the smell brings Roland, and Roland opens the panel and sees a 30 where a 20 should be and goes very quiet. He holds it up for Pastor Dave like evidence at a trial. Pastor Dave looks at the breaker, then at the only other person with a key to that closet. "Let's chat this week," he says. Tom, from the floor, says he's fine. Nobody asked.

Eight AM. You have twenty minutes of office quiet before the volunteers arrive and quiet becomes a memory. You use it the way you always do: on a problem that is technically not urgent and therefore the only kind of problem you ever actually get to finish.

The donut invoice is wrong. The standing order is eighteen dozen at the negotiated rate. This invoice says eighteen dozen at a rate that is not the negotiated rate, plus a "weekend handling fee" that does not exist in the contract, plus tax on the fee that does not exist. The total is $340 over. You know it's $340 over because you keep the contract in a folder and the rate in your head and the previous eleven invoices in a spreadsheet that nobody asked you to build.

The donuts are already here. The donuts are already on the lobby table. People are already eating the disputed donuts. This is the problem with food: the leverage is gone the moment it arrives. You cannot un-donut a church.

## A

You call the vendor at 8:04 on a Sunday, which is aggressive, and you read the contract rate back to them line by line, which is more aggressive. The kid who answers does not know what a "negotiated rate" is. You ask for the owner. The owner knows exactly what a negotiated rate is. The "handling fee" evaporates. A credit appears on next month's invoice. It took four minutes and it saved $340 and it is the single most satisfying thing that will happen to you all day, and you are the only person who will ever know it happened.

## B

You approve the invoice, absorb the $340 as the cost of donuts that are already in people's mouths, and put a calendar block on Monday at 8:00 labeled "DONUT CONTRACT — RENEGOTIATE." You will keep that block. You will renegotiate the contract. The handling fee will never appear again. You lost $340 today and closed the leak forever, which is the most administrator trade there is: a small public loss for a permanent private win nobody will ever see.

## C

You approve it. You file it. You tell yourself $340 is not worth a Sunday-morning phone call, which is true. What you forget is that the handling fee is not a mistake. It is a test. It comes back next month, and the month after, and by the time Brenda flags it during reconciliation, the church has paid the fee that does not exist eleven times, and the question is no longer "why is this invoice wrong." The question is "why did *you* keep approving it." You do not have a great answer for the version of the question that has your initials on it.

## D:win

You don't dispute the $340. You *relocate* it. You move the overage out of the catering line, where it looks like a mistake, and into a category called "MISC — EVENT FLOW," where it looks like a decision. EVENT FLOW is a line you created. EVENT FLOW absorbs things. The invoice now reconciles cleanly, the donut budget stays pristine, and the $340 simply becomes part of the gentle, breathing fund that smooths the church's rough edges before anyone feels them. Nobody questions a line that's already balanced. The donuts are paid for. The books are clean. You are very good at this, and that is precisely the problem you are not thinking about yet.

## D:lose

You reclassify the $340 into "MISC — EVENT FLOW," which works, the way it always works, except today is the Sunday Brenda decided to "tidy up" the categories before quarter-end. She opens EVENT FLOW. EVENT FLOW is larger than she expected. EVENT FLOW contains a donut handling fee, a parking-cone replacement, a "vendor goodwill" entry with no vendor named, and three years of small, confident roundings, all balanced, all neat, all yours. Brenda does not understand what she is looking at. Brenda understands that she does not understand it, which is worse. She prints it. Brenda always prints it.

Turnover. You're in your office, between services, eating a granola bar that is functioning as both breakfast and lunch, when the text comes in from home.

It's a photo. Your kid, dressed up, holding the thing they made for the thing. The thing that is happening today. The thing you knew was happening today, in the abstract, the way you know the sun is a star — true, established, somehow not load-bearing in the moment. The caption is gentle. That's what makes it land. There's no guilt trip in it. There doesn't need to be. The absence of a guilt trip *is* the guilt trip, refined to its purest form.

You are here on your day off. That's the part nobody outside this building understands. Sunday isn't even your job — Sunday is pastoral, Sunday is front-of-house — and yet here you are, because the chair count was twelve short and somebody had to be the person who noticed, and the person who notices is never home for the thing.

Second service starts in fourteen minutes. The building is, for once, basically fine. There is no fire. There is only the absence of a fire and a photo on your phone, and the two of them are, right now, the loudest thing in the room.

## A

You find Roland and you find Tom and you say the sentence you almost never say: "I need to step out for ninety minutes. You two have the building." Roland nods. Tom, handed real responsibility for the first time, stands a little straighter and says "we've got it" with a gravity that tells you he's wanted to be asked for years. You drive to the thing. You make the thing. Your kid sees you walk in and their whole face does something, and the building does not burn down without you, which is a small, devastating lesson you will spend the rest of the week pretending you didn't learn.

## B

You text back a heart and "so sorry — work emergency, I'll make it up to you," and you stay, because staying is the thing you know how to do and leaving is the thing you don't. There is no emergency. You invent small tasks to make the staying feel justified — you re-file the catering folder, you re-count the offering bags — and each invented task is a tiny brick in a wall you are building between yourself and the people who actually need you. Second service runs fine. It would have run fine without you. You knew that when you sent the heart.

## C

You don't text. Texting is how you've gotten out of every hard conversation for three years, and you're tired of being good at it. You call. You let them talk. You actually hear the disappointment instead of skimming it for the part you can fix. And then you do the one thing that matters more than showing up today, which is making a specific promise you can actually keep — not "soon," not "I'll try," but a day and a time, written down, treated like a vendor contract, because the people you love deserve at least the rigor you give the donut guy. You keep it, too. That's the whole point. You keep it.

## D:win

You text "be there in twenty!" and you mean it, and then the second-service offering count comes up four bags short and you decide that's a twenty-minute problem, and it is, but solving it surfaces a deposit discrepancy, which is a thirty-minute problem, and somewhere in there twenty minutes quietly becomes the entire second service. You make it to the thing, late, for the very end. You catch the last four minutes. Your kid is glad you came. You are glad you came. Nobody says the word "late." Everybody is thinking the word "late." You got away with it today. You will not always get away with it, and the granola-bar part of you knows exactly which Sundays are spending which.

## D:lose

You text "be there in twenty!" and then you stay to fix one more thing, and one more thing, and the thing after that, and twenty minutes becomes ninety, and you walk in as everyone is leaving, as your kid is carrying the made thing back to the car, deflated in the specific way of a child who stopped checking the door an hour ago. You did not get away with it. The made thing goes on the fridge anyway, because they are kinder than you've earned, and every time you open the fridge for a week you will see it, and it will say, in crayon, the thing that "MISC — EVENT FLOW" can never quite cover.

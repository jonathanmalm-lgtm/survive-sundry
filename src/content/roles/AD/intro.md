Your alarm goes off at 6:00. You have seventeen unread emails from last night. You read all seventeen before getting out of bed, which is its own kind of devotional. Three require responses before 8 AM. You draft two of them in your head while the coffee brews. Someone in your house asks what you're thinking about. "Nothing," you say. Then, two seconds later, with total sincerity: "Catering invoices."

Sunday is technically not your domain. Sunday is pastoral. Sunday is creative. Sunday is front-of-house — lights, lobby, the part people clap for. You are the back. But the back runs the front, and everyone knows it, and Pastor Dave knows it better than anyone, which is precisely why he never has to say it.

Which is why you are going in at 7:00 AM. On your day off. Because the folding-chair count for Service 2 is twelve short and nobody else noticed, and nobody else was ever going to notice, because noticing is the entire job and the job has exactly one person in it.

You've known since 2011 that 7:00 AM is late in church world. You will arrive at 6:58.

Welcome to Execution Church. Today is Sunday. The mission statement says "Make disciples or else…" You have read those three dots every morning for three years. You still don't know who they're threatening. You suspect it's you.

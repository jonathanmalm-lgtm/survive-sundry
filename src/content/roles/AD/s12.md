Twenty minutes after second service. Everyone is standing. Nobody has eaten since the huddle donuts four hours ago. This is not a meeting with food. This is the meeting where you find out what people actually thought.

The campus pastor goes first. Hit: both services ran. Miss: he spent turnover in the lobby and missed his check-in window with the team. He looks around the circle.

Then someone says it. First Impressions reports, gently, that "the lobby felt a little cold this morning," and then — because there is always an *and then* — adds that a guest tweeted "ice church 🥶," and that the tweet has fourteen likes, and produces, from a folder, a printout of the tweet. Fourteen likes, screenshotted, in a church meeting. The room turns, slowly, the way a room turns, toward the person whose job is the building. That's you. You have lived a day that contained a dying capacitor, a phantom $1,400 invoice, a fire panel in fault, a man named Danny paid from an envelope marked EVENT FLOW, forty cars in a lot that wasn't yours, and a connection card you cannot stop thinking about. You nod slowly at the printout. Inside, you are somewhere very far from this lobby.

## A

You take it cleanly: "The lobby was cold. The east unit's capacitor failed overnight — same sound it made last year, same part. I had it running by 9:40, but the room ran behind the first crowd. New part goes in Tuesday and I'm putting the unit on a service contract so it can't surprise us again." The room exhales. The campus pastor stops writing. There is something almost suspicious about an administrator who answers a complaint with a part number and a Tuesday, and the suspicion resolves, in everyone present, into the quiet relief of realizing the building has exactly one adult in it and the adult is awake.

## B

Clean and confident: you name the morning's wins — both services covered, the overflow handled, the door re-staffed on the fly — and *then* you name the lobby, attached to its cause and its fix, so the cold becomes one managed line in a morning full of saves rather than the headline. The room sees a person who clearly runs something. First Impressions nods along, mildly reabsorbing their own complaint. You do not gloat. Gloating is how you'd give it all back. You simply let the frame hold, and it holds.

## C

You say the HVAC vendor was delayed and the room ran behind, and you stop talking. It's true. It's also a shape with the load-bearing parts removed, and the campus pastor can feel the removed parts even if he can't see them. He nods and moves on. But "the vendor was delayed" is the kind of answer that invites a follow-up later, in a smaller room, where "which vendor, and what did we pay them" is a question you would much rather not field alone. You bought quiet now at the price of a conversation later. You are usually better at pricing things.

## D:win

"The lobby went cold," you say, with the flat calm of a man reading a maintenance log, "because the unit was flagged first thing this morning. There was a note on the thermostat. It was removed during setup before facilities could act on it." You do not raise your voice. You do not say a name. You simply let your gaze rest, for exactly one beat too long, on Brenda — who removed the note, who removed it for looking "messy," who is now reviewing her entire morning in real time. The blame slides off you and lands, soft and permanent, on a sticky note nobody can produce. Brenda's mouth opens and then closes. Next Sunday, every note you write survives the morning untouched. You have weaponized stationery.

## D:lose

You deliver the sticky-note theory with total administrative calm, gaze resting on Brenda — and Brenda, it turns out, has the timestamps. Brenda reconciles this church for a living; Brenda keeps a log on the petty-cash lid and a record of every change she makes; and Brenda calmly says, "I did move a note at 9:20. I have a photo. The note said the unit needed *attention,* not that it was *fixed* — and the thermostat already read 62 when I got there at 7:40." A pause. "When did the unit actually fail?" The room turns back toward you. The campus pastor tilts his head and asks, mildly, "Was the note ever going to fix the unit?" And it would not have, and everyone can tell it would not have, and you realize you tried to bury a cold lobby in the one place in the building where someone keeps better records than you do.

Around the room: tech reports a "supervisory thing on the fire panel, handled." Worship says second service "had something." Kids ministry describes a diaper situation in nine words and the room receives them in silence. First Impressions says the door felt warm but the lobby felt cold; nobody claims the lobby a second time. Someone says the haze was "a lot today." Nobody claims the haze.

"Same time next week."

Four seconds.

Brenda moves first.

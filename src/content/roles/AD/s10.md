Turnover into the afternoon. Your office. Three things have landed on your desk in the last ten minutes, and they are all, underneath, the same thing: each one would cost the church real money through the real process, slowly, painfully, with forms.

One: the approved HVAC vendor — the one you went around this morning — has emailed a $1,400 "emergency diagnostic" invoice for a truck that never arrived, and they will make it loud if it's disputed in front of the board. Two: the coffee vendor shorted you four bags and wants payment in full anyway, and the relationship is worth keeping. Three: you are, as of this morning's cash count, $600 down in petty cash and somebody is going to reconcile that box on Tuesday.

Three problems. And one beautiful, quiet, breathing solution sitting in your spreadsheet under a category you invented, fed for three years on roundings and "miscellaneous" and one suspiciously generous line called MISC — EVENT FLOW. It has enough in it to make all three of these problems simply not exist. Nobody approved EVENT FLOW. Nobody knows the exact size of EVENT FLOW. EVENT FLOW is yours, and it was built for exactly this, for the Sundays when the right way is too slow and the church needs a wizard instead of a process.

Your cursor is hovering over it. It has been hovering over it for three years.

## A

You close the spreadsheet. You solve each one straight: you dispute the phantom invoice in writing with the documentation you always keep, you pay the coffee vendor the real amount with a real note about the short bags, and you flag the petty-cash gap to be backfilled through the proper account with your name on the slip. It's slower. It's a worse afternoon. It surfaces a couple of awkward costs in the open where people can see them. But every dollar can now be explained by you, to anyone, in daylight, without your stomach dropping — and you'd forgotten how light that feels, defending numbers you don't have to flinch about.

## B

You decide all three can wait, because right now you cannot face the forms, and you close the laptop. None of them waits well. The phantom invoice escalates to the board because that's what phantom invoices do when ignored. The coffee vendor stops returning calls. The petty-cash gap is still a gap on Tuesday, only now it's a gap with two days of aging on it and no explanation prepared. You traded a hard afternoon for a worse week, which is the one trade the back of the house can never afford, because the back of the house has no one behind it to catch what it drops.

## C

You walk down the hall, knock on Pastor Dave's open door, sit down, and tell him the truth — all of it, the real cost of the HVAC, the vendor short, the cash gap, and, while you're at it, the existence of a discretionary cushion you've quietly maintained to smooth exactly these moments. You brace for it. It doesn't come. Pastor Dave listens the whole way through, and then he says, "I always wondered how you made the rough Sundays disappear." A pause. "Let's make it a real line. With my signature on it. So it's never just yours." It is the most exposed you have felt in years and the lightest you have felt in longer. The wizard, it turns out, was allowed to ask for help.

## D:win

You open EVENT FLOW and you go to work, and it is, frankly, a kind of art. The phantom invoice gets quietly paid down to nuisance level from "vendor goodwill." The coffee short gets absorbed into "hospitality." The $600 cash gap gets papered with a reclassification so clean it reconciles itself. Money moves between line items with the calm authority of a man who has done this a hundred times, because you have, and by the time you close the laptop, all three problems are not solved so much as *retired* — gone, smooth, no paper trail, no flinch. The fund even regrows by Friday off a rounding you set in motion last month. Brenda reconciles Tuesday and finds nothing, because there is nothing to find, because you are the best there has ever been at making nothing to find. You are an unsung wizard. The church runs on a magic only you can see.

## D:lose

You open EVENT FLOW and you start moving money with that same calm authority, and it is going perfectly — phantom invoice handled, coffee short absorbed — right up until you remember, one beat too late, that Ron emailed Brenda this week asking for "the detailed line items," and that Brenda, being Brenda, is already in the books, *right now,* tidying for quarter-end. By the time you've reclassified the third problem, Brenda has reconciled EVENT FLOW against three years of receipts and found a $4,200 discrepancy with your initials threaded through it like a signature. She prints it. She does not ask you first. She walks it to Pastor Dave, who reads it slowly, and then asks you, very mildly, in the tone he uses for the things that are not mild at all: "What has MISC — EVENT FLOW been covering, exactly? For three years?" And you, who have an explanation for everything, who have *always* had an explanation for everything, discover that you do not have a comfortable one for this.

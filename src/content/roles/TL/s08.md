Seventeen minutes of turnover. Your checklist has fourteen items and you have time for ten. You are on item three when you notice him: a kid, maybe six, alone on the empty stage. Not near the stage. *On* it. He has discovered the worship leader's mic stand and is slow-dancing with it. Now he's drifting toward the keyboard — your keyboard, the live one, the one worth more than his future bicycle — and he has just learned that it makes sounds.

You sweep the lobby. Not one adult is tracking this child. The band is on break. The stage is his kingdom and he is an unsupervised king with sticky hands moving toward forty channels of expensive, fragile faith.

## A

You leave the booth, cross the room, and make eye contact with the kid. The kid makes eye contact back. The kid runs — not off the stage, *deeper* onto it. You are now chasing a six-year-old around a drum kit during turnover while wearing all black like a very unsuccessful ninja. Cody is watching from the booth. Cody is filming from the booth. Cody will have this footage forever.

## B

Your voice booms through the whole building: "Could the parents of a small boy on the main stage please come to the stage." The boy's mother appears, mortified. The boy does not share her mortification. The boy waves at the now-watching lobby. The lobby waves back. You have spent precious venue-mic capital, and the legend of the waving boy is now larger than anything you will accomplish today.

## C

The keyboard dies mid-note. The kid looks at the keyboard. The kid looks at his own hands with the dawning horror of a boy who believes he has broken something with his mind. He presses a key. Silence. He accepts this cosmic injustice and pivots immediately toward the drum kit. You have solved one problem and personally escorted him to the next one.

## D:win

You reach into your tech bag and produce a 9-volt battery. You crouch to his level and explain, with great seriousness, that you are about to teach him the most important skill in all of audio production. You demonstrate. He does it without hesitation. The half-second after contact produces the purest, most complete expression of surprise you have ever witnessed on a human face. He pulls back. He stares at the battery. He stares at you. "It works," you say.

He spends the entire second service quietly and reverently testing every 9-volt in your bag, sorting the live from the dead with total focus. He has, frankly, contributed meaningfully to your inventory management. You will never tell his father the methodology.

## D:lose

The kid tells his father immediately after service, with the radiant joy of a boy sharing the single best thing that has ever happened to him, pointing at you across the lobby the entire time. The father follows the finger. Pastor Dave, nearby, follows the father. Three sets of eyes arrive on you at once. Pastor Dave's expression cycles through several distinct emotional weather systems and lands on one you have never seen him use before. He mouths something. You cannot be certain what. You are certain it was not "well done."

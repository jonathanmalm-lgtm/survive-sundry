The team huddle is at 9:00 AM in the lobby. You are here because it is mandatory. You are mentally still in the booth, watching it on the camera feed in your mind.

The First Impressions leader steps forward and names what everyone already knows: you're all short-staffed today. The holes are real. "But," he says, pausing for effect, "God won't give you more than you can handle." He says it like Scripture. It is Scripture. It's 1 Corinthians 10:13, which is about resisting temptation, not running four tech positions with three humans. You let it go. Volunteers need Jesus, not exegesis.

Pastor Dave produces the $5 Chick-fil-A gift card for the one volunteer closest to burnout. Today it's Terry. His wife just had a baby last week and he is here anyway, in the parking lot every Sunday, either deeply committed to the mission or quietly escaping the newborn — you have decided not to ask which. Terry tears up over the gift card. Waffle fries can carry a man a long way.

Then Pastor introduces Greg — former worship pastor at a well-known church that recently imploded after their pastor's moral failure. Greg is here to "observe and encourage." Greg is wearing a jacket that costs more than your subwoofer. Greg glances at your gear notes on the table and says, with real warmth, "Oh, you're tech? We'd just finished upgrading our whole system before everything happened. Full Dante network, fiber runs, the works."

## A

Greg turns out to be genuinely helpful. You walk away with three legitimately useful ideas and a strange new ache about what you could do with an actual budget. You also burned eleven minutes while your volunteers sat unsupervised at a powered-up board, which is its own kind of risk.

## B

Clean. Professional. Greg reads it as focus and is quietly impressed. You are back in the booth before he finishes his sentence, where you belong, in the dark, with the machines that love you.

## C

You tell him the truth: the held-together-with-gaff-tape truth, the "this HDMI run is technically held up by a zip tie and faith" truth. Greg is genuinely impressed. He asks if you've ever considered consulting. You will think about that sentence at red lights for the next several days.

## D:win

The room goes quiet. Greg's smile holds at exactly its current size for one second too long. Then it breaks — and a real laugh arrives, the kind that takes four full seconds to surface. "No," he says, wiping his eye. "Dante is an audio networking protocol." "Right," you say. "Obviously." He finds you in the booth after service. "That," he says, "was the worst and the best question anyone has asked me all year." You file it under win.

## D:lose

The room goes quiet. Greg does not laugh. Pastor Dave physically stands up straighter, activating a setting he reserves for specific occasions. The huddle moves on. Three volunteers near the back become extremely interested in their own shoes. Pastor Dave finds you during turnover and says three sentences. You will remember all three for the rest of your natural life, and possibly into the next one.

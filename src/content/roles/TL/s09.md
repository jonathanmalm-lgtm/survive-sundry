Second service. Worship is locked in, the room is engaged, and then the little red dot on the encoder turns into a little gray dot, which in your line of work is the color of death. The stream is down. The room has no idea. The internet has every idea. The chat is already filling: "audio cut out?" "is it just me" "buffering 😣" "WE LOVE YOU FROM OHIO is it back."

You have, by the platform's count, 1,412 live viewers. You have long suspected that at least three of these are Russian bot accounts that joined the channel on the same day in 2021, never comment, and watch every service start to finish. You have given them names. But you also know, with certainty, that one of the real ones is the campus pastor's 74-year-old mother, who watches every single week without fail.

## A

You go in. You kill the encoder, re-key the stream, restart the bonded connection, and coax it back to life in four minutes that age you four years. For those four minutes you are not watching the room, the levels, or the lighting. Something small drifts off in the mix while you're heads-down. Nobody in the building notices. You notice. You will think about it tonight instead of sleeping.

## B

You throw up the holding card — "We're having a technical moment. The Word is still being preached. Notes linked below." — and you put Cody on the chat to reassure people and drop the sermon outline. People are gracious. Several thank Cody by name. You fix the stream properly during the closing song. It is not flashy. It is just correct, which is the highest compliment your job ever receives and never out loud.

## C

You make a theological bet that the only true viewers are bots and bots do not file complaints. You are 99% right. The 1% is the campus pastor's mother, who picks up her landline and calls her son during the closing prayer to tell him the church "went out again." He walks straight to the booth. "My mom watches every week," he says. You did not fully appreciate that his mom watches every week. You think about the Russian bots. You say nothing about the Russian bots.

## D:win

You cut to last Sunday's worship footage and label it LIVE. It is a flawless deception. The two songs happen to match closely enough, the lighting is similar, and the congregation on screen is worshipping just as hard as the one in the room. The chat settles. "ok it's back 🙌". You quietly rebuild the real stream underneath and slip back to live before anyone is the wiser. You have committed broadcast fraud in the house of God and it was, technically, your finest work all morning.

## D:lose

You cut to last week's footage. What you forget, in your panic, is that last week the worship leader wore a very memorable mustard-yellow sweater, and today they are in black. Somewhere in Ohio, a woman who watches very closely types: "wait… isn't this the same as last week?" She screenshots. She compares. She posts both side by side captioned "does my church FAKE the live worship???" It travels. By Monday it has a Reddit thread. Pastor Dave forwards you the link with no message, just a calendar invite, and the invite has no body text, only a time.

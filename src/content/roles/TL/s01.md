You're the first one in the building. You always are. You have a key, a code, and a complicated relationship with the alarm panel. You go straight to the booth and power everything up in the sequence you laminated and taped inside the booth door after the incident last February that you do not discuss.

Everything comes up clean. Except the subwoofer. It makes a sound like a disappointed sigh and then goes quiet. Not broken-quiet. Suspicious-quiet. You put your hand on the cabinet. You feel a faint hum, the hum of a machine deciding whether today is the day.

Sound check is at 8:30. The worship leader is doing three Elevation songs that are 40% low end by volume. Without the sub, those songs will sound like a podcast.

## A

Forty minutes on your knees with a multimeter. The amp card is failing — you know exactly how badly, which is its own kind of curse. You can plan around it. You've also just spent forty minutes you did not have, and the rest of the morning will be a sprint. But you know the truth, and knowing the truth is the only thing that lets you sleep, which you don't do anyway.

## B

Professional. A paper trail. The vendor will not respond until Monday at 10:14 AM with the phrase "happy to take a look!" But when Pastor Dave asks what happened, you will have receipts, timestamps, and the moral high ground, which at this church is worth approximately one Chick-fil-A gift card.

## C

Clean. Decisive. The worship leader notices in the first eight bars and gives you a look across the room that says *where is my bass*. The low end is, in the words of no one, "present but polite." Ron in row four will love it. Ron will find you afterward to say it was the most reverent the music has sounded in years. You will not enjoy agreeing with Ron.

## D:win

There is a recalled amp card in your drawer. The recall notice used the words "thermal event." You kept it because throwing away functional electronics feels like a sin and because some part of you knew this morning was coming.

You drop it in. You bring the gain up slow, like coaxing a horse. It holds. It more than holds. The low end on that first song is so deep and so clean that a baby in the second row stops crying and stares at the stage like it has seen the throne room. Three people text the worship leader to ask if you got new gear. You did not get new gear. You committed a small act of audio crime and got away with it.

## D:lose

There is a recalled amp card in your drawer. The recall notice used the words "thermal event."

It survives sound check. It survives the first two songs. Halfway through the third it emits a frequency that has no technical name but every engineer would recognize on sight, followed by a thin, honest ribbon of smoke. The bass note that exits in its final moment is so concentrated that a kid running laps in the front row gets knocked flat by the displacement of air. Not tripped. Not pushed. *Knocked over by sound.* He will tell this story for the rest of his life and no one will fully believe him. His mother is already walking toward the booth. You are on a headset. It is 10:31 AM on a Sunday.

Call time is 7:30. Cody — seventeen, recruited from student ministry three weeks ago, still thinks the job is cool — is here early on slides. Kayla rolls in at 7:31 looking like she was up until 4 AM, because she was up until 4 AM. The fourth volunteer never confirmed on Planning Center. You saw the empty slot Thursday. You chose to believe it would resolve itself, the way you believe the subwoofer healed itself, the way you believe a lot of things on Thursdays.

Three people. Four positions. Sound check in an hour.

## A

You are now running audio with your hands and lighting with your peripheral vision and your soul with neither. It will cost you by second service — you can feel the deficit being drawn against your account in real time. But the booth runs clean and nothing collapses, which is the entire job description, printed nowhere, thanked by no one.

## B

Cody is now running slides and lights. His lighting during the offering features an ambitious amount of deep red and one unplanned moment of strobe that a few congregants will describe later as "a lot." His slides arrive so late that the room has already finished singing the line before it appears. The worship leader turns and looks at the booth. Pastor Dave looks at the booth. Two different looks. Both are addressed to you. Both will require conversations this week.

## C

"omw" at 8:14. Arrives at 9:02 smelling faintly of a Saturday that went well. At 10:34, mid-service, texts you from fifteen feet away: "hey which one is the lighting board." You do not answer. You cannot answer. You are holding the entire morning together with a USB hub and a prayer.

## D:win

Kids ministry is now short a body, but they flex and survive. The kids minister texts you at 8:47: "did you take my volunteer." It is not a question. You write back: "I'll explain later." She replies with a single period. Not a word. Not an emoji. A period. You have made a powerful enemy and you have a fully staffed booth. On this particular Sunday, you would make the trade again.

## D:lose

Bryson is four years old and has spent all morning waiting for a door to be left open. Your staffing crime opens the door. Bryson walks out of the kids wing unescorted — through the lobby, past the welcome desk, through the auditorium doors, down the center aisle, and up the side stairs onto the stage during the third song. Bryson is now facing three hundred people. Bryson waves. Three hundred people wave back. The worship leader stops singing. Pastor Dave is already moving from the back like a man who has rehearsed this in dreams.

It takes three adults forty-five seconds to extract Bryson. You will go to the kids minister immediately after service. You will not make excuses. You will bring the good coffee, not the lobby coffee.

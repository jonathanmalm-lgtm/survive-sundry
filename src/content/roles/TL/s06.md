Third song. The room is locked in. Everything is going suspiciously smoothly, which is the exact condition under which everything goes wrong.

ProPresenter advances. Instead of the verse, the twelve-foot screen behind the worship leader displays a meme. Not just any meme. The single finest meme you have ever produced — a devastating, perfect, deeply disrespectful meme about Pastor Dave that you sent to exactly two trusted coworkers in a group chat titled, regrettably, "do not screenshot." It is being screenshotted right now by a congregation of three hundred.

Cody fixes the slide. Worship continues. But it's out there. Pastor Dave is already walking toward the booth at the unhurried pace of a man who has all the time in the world and intends to use it on you. He reaches the door. He looks at you. He says, "What was that?"

## A

He listens. He does not interrupt. When you finish, he's quiet a moment. "Who else is in the chat?" he asks. You tell him. He nods. He walks away. Three steps out, he turns back. "It was funny," he says. "Don't do that again." Then he's gone. You will not fully trust that compliment. The compliment is real. You still won't trust it.

## B

He looks at you. He looks at the screen, now innocently displaying lyrics. He looks back at you. "A file," he repeats. "Yes," you say. He nods slowly, the nod of a man choosing to accept an answer he does not believe in order to get back on stage. "Clean it up." He walks away. The reprieve is temporary and you both know it.

## C

It's technically not a lie — Cody was loading files this morning, and a thing did get into the deck. You are simply declining to mention whose thing it was. You let Pastor Dave arrive at the wrong conclusion under his own power. Cody hears the whole thing from six feet away. Cody looks at you. You look away first. Cody will not bring this up today. Cody will bring this up at a time and place entirely of his own choosing, possibly years from now, possibly in his wedding toast.

## D:win

Pastor Dave's face moves from confused to concerned. "I'll talk to him," he says, and walks off to address a crime the youth pastor did not commit. As a bonus, he finally institutes the shared-equipment policy the building has needed for two years. Good came from evil. You tell no one. The youth pastor never finds out it was you. You got away with it, and it cost you something you can't quite name but can definitely feel.

## D:lose

The youth pastor figures it out within forty-eight hours. He texts you Monday: "really?" One word. You draft and delete six replies before sending "can we grab coffee?" He replies: "sure." The coffee happens Wednesday. It is the most uncomfortable forty-five minutes of your month. He is not angry. He is disappointed in the specific, surgical way that is so much worse than angry, and he lets the silences run long on purpose.

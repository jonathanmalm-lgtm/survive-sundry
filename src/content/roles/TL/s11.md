Services are done. You're running the post-service diagnostic in the slowly emptying room. On his way out, Cody refills your water bottle without being asked and doesn't say one word about the subwoofer, the meme, the stream, the kid, the battery, or the haze. He just sets it down and goes. You drink it. It is the best water you have ever had. It is the exact same water you drink every day. Context is everything.

Then a man approaches. Fifty-five, large, with wispy red hair that is doing its absolute best. He introduces himself and announces he wants to join your tech team. He doesn't need training. He used to tour with DC Talk. You ask in what capacity. He becomes vague about the capacity. What is clear is this: he was, at some point, in proximity to DC Talk, he has been in rooms with professional equipment, and he would like to start next Sunday.

## A

He takes it. He does not look thrilled about it. He shadows the following Sunday with the air of a man humoring a formality beneath his station. By the end of second service, you are genuinely unsure which of you is being trained. He keeps saying "back on the road we used to—" and never finishes the sentence.

## B

He sits down next Sunday with the confidence of a man who has been in rooms like this. During the second song he does something to the mix that may have made sense in 1995 on a tour bus outside Tulsa. The congregation tilts its collective head. He raises a hand before you can reach the board. "I got it," he says. He does, eventually, get it. The intervening ninety seconds take a year off your life.

## C

His Facebook is public. His Facebook is *extensive.* He was definitely around DC Talk — he's in several photos, usually slightly out of frame. You find exactly one where he is fully in frame. He is holding a clipboard. You study the clipboard photo far longer than a reasonable person would. You text him Monday: "Welcome to the team." You still don't know what the clipboard was for. You think about the clipboard sometimes.

## D:win

You are in Cancun. You receive three texts on your first Sunday off in two years. Cody: "it's going ok." Kayla: "he's interesting." The campus pastor: "who is the large man in the booth." You place your phone face-down on the beach chair. You pick up a drink with an umbrella in it. You have made peace with whatever is unfolding in that booth, for today, only today, and the peace tastes like pineapple.

## D:lose

You are in Cancun. You receive seven texts, two missed calls, and a voice memo from Kayla that is three minutes and forty-seven seconds long. You listen to the whole thing. It describes, in significant technical detail, an event from second service that Kayla believes you must know about immediately. You are standing on a beach. You do know about it immediately. You finish your drink. You book the earlier flight home.

9:51 AM. Nine minutes to first service. The worship leader wants "atmosphere." The camera director wants "atmosphere." Atmosphere means haze — that fine theatrical fog that makes light beams look like the literal presence of God descending on a midsize suburban auditorium.

The problem is physics. The haze machine and the sanctuary smoke detector have a long and bitter history. There is a level of haze that reads beautifully on camera, and there is a level of haze that summons the fire department, and these two levels are approximately four percent apart on the same dial. You have danced on this knife before. February was about haze. February is why you laminated things.

## A

You run it at a responsible level. The light beams are visible. They are not transcendent, but they are present, like a low-key blessing. The camera director gives you a look that says *more.* You give the camera director a look that says *I would like to keep my job.* The look-conversation ends in a draw. The room looks good. Nobody evacuates. This is, by your standards, a triumph.

## B

You call facilities. Facilities is one man named Roland and Roland is currently elbow-deep in a toilet in the east wing. Roland says he'll "get to the panel after first service." First service is the service that needs the haze. You run no haze. The shot is flat but legal. Roland texts you at 12:40 PM: "panel's good now 👍". Thank you, Roland.

## C

You skip the haze entirely. The lights have nothing to catch, so they just sort of exist, naked and embarrassed in the open air. The worship leader squints at the stage all through the first song, trying to figure out what feels off. They will never quite identify it. They will simply sense that this Sunday lacked glory, and somewhere deep down, they will know it was you.

## D:win

You find a sandwich bag in the green room snack drawer. You climb the ladder. You seal the smoke detector inside it with the tenderness of a man defusing a bomb, which, legally, is not far off. Then you open the haze valve like Moses parting a sea of vapor.

The room becomes a cathedral. The light beams are SOLID. People walk in and audibly gasp. A first-time guest later writes on a connection card that she "felt the Spirit the second she entered." That was haze. That was you and a Ziploc bag. You remove the bag during the closing prayer and no one is ever the wiser. You have sinned beautifully.

## D:lose

You seal the detector and open the valve and for ninety glorious seconds you are a god of weather. Then the haze does what haze does — it wanders. It drifts off the stage, into the lobby, and finds the OTHER detector, the one you forgot about, the one not currently wearing a sandwich bag.

The alarm is louder than worship has ever been. Strobes. A recorded voice instructing everyone to walk, not run, to the nearest exit. Six hundred people file into the parking lot, where Wendell is already calmly directing evacuation traffic as though he has been waiting his whole life for this exact moment. The fire department arrives. They find the detector. They find the bag. They find you, standing next to a haze machine, dressed entirely in black, looking exactly as guilty as you are.

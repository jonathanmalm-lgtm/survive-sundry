8:05 AM. ProPresenter is open, the set is built, the verses are checked, the world is briefly in order. Then your phone lights up with a text from the worship leader: *"hey!! dropping song 2 — God put a new one on my heart at like 6 this morning, can we add it? it's in B, has a click, the bridge does a thing, you'll figure it out 🙏🙏"*

You'll figure it out. Three words that have aged you more than any other three words in ministry. The song is not built. The key is wrong for the tracks. The whole band is standing on stage waiting for a chart that exists only in the worship leader's heart and possibly the mind of God. You have about twenty-two minutes. You are, once again, the bottleneck for someone else's spontaneity.

## A

You build it. You key-shift the tracks, you import the lyrics, you fix the bridge "thing," and you have it ready with ninety seconds to spare. Nobody knows what it took. The worship leader gives you a thumbs up across the room like the song simply appeared, summoned by faith. You take the win privately. You always take the win privately.

## B

You build it, and then you do the thing nobody does: you make a clean chart and airdrop it to the whole band so they aren't guessing the bridge live. The music director nearly weeps. Kayla covers slides without being asked. For one shining moment the team functions like a team and not like seven people who happen to be standing near each other. This is leadership. No one will know it happened. You'll know.

## C

"Ah man — that one's not cleared in CCLI yet, can't legally project it." This is a lie. The song is extremely cleared. But you have invoked the one law no one at this church will fact-check, because CCLI is your kingdom and you are its only citizen. The worship leader sighs and goes back to the original set. You have purchased twenty-two minutes of peace with a small, deniable sin.

## D:win

You build the song flawlessly. Then, into the worship leader's confidence monitor only — the private screen, the one only they can see — you change exactly one word. The line "I'm no longer a slave to fear" now reads "I'm no longer a slave to beer."

Mid-song, the worship leader reads it, audibly chokes, recovers, and spends the rest of the bridge fighting for their life against a laugh. Only the booth knows why. Cody knows. Cody will never tell. The worship leader corners you after, eyes watering, and whispers "I will end you," and then texts you at midnight: "ok that was actually really funny." You have established, gently, that 6 AM audibles have a cost.

## D:lose

You change exactly one word in the confidence monitor. What you forget, in your sleep-deprived hubris, is that the confidence monitor and the main screen are mirrored this morning because you were rushing.

Three hundred people read "I'm no longer a slave to beer" off the main screen. Three hundred people sing "I'm no longer a slave to beer." They sing it with *conviction.* A man in row nine raises both hands. Ron writes it down. Someone films it. By Tuesday the clip has a caption and the caption is not kind. Pastor Dave does not yell. Pastor Dave simply asks, in a voice with no floor beneath it, "Was that the projector, or was that a person."

The sermon is running. You step out of the booth for the first time since 6:52 AM in search of water and a single moment of peace. You find neither. You find Ron.

Ron has attended Execution Church for eleven years. He stops you with a specific energy — not angry, just prepared, the way a man is prepared who has been composing this conversation in the shower for a week. Today's feedback is technical. The mix was too loud. The bass was disorienting. The lighting in the second song was, and he uses this word, "assaultive." He produces a folded paper from his shirt pocket. It is a printed list. It is numbered.

Then Ron tells you what he tithes each month. It is, to the dollar, your entire salary. From one man. You do not currently know what to do with your face. He waits.

## A

You read it. Item 7 is a genuinely accurate note about a feedback loop on the stage-left wedge that you have been meaning to fix since Lent. Ron is, on occasion, infuriatingly correct. You thank him. He nods like a man who has been correct for eleven years and is rarely thanked for it.

## B

Ron does not know how to use the church app. You spend six minutes of the sermon teaching him to navigate to a feedback form that routes to the general inbox, an address that has not been opened by a living person since 2019. Ron feels heard. Ron has not been heard. You have purchased a week.

## C

Ron looks at the list. Ron looks at you. Ron takes the list back, folds it along its original creases, and returns it to his shirt pocket. "I've been here eleven years," he says, and walks away. The list is not gone. The list has merely returned to its holster. The list will ride again.

## D:win

You have invented this research wholesale. Ron stares at you. You watch a terrible thought enter Ron: that he, a significant tither, may have been emotionally manipulated into generosity by decibels for over a decade. He takes his list back slowly. He walks away thinking. Ron will research this. Ron will find conflicting information from sources of varying credibility. Ron will return next Sunday with printed articles, possibly highlighted, possibly laminated by Wendell.

## D:lose

Ron walks directly to the campus pastor and reports, verbatim, that the tech leader admitted to cranking the volume to manipulate the offering. The campus pastor finds you in the booth during turnover. He does not raise his voice. "Did you tell Ron we turn up the music to manipulate giving?" "I said *increase emotional engagement,*" you clarify, which does not help. He holds your gaze a moment. "Same time next week," he says. It is not a warm same time next week.

Turnover. You're scrubbing the multicam to pull a worship clip for the social team. The booth cameras run on motion-trigger from the moment you arm the system at 6 AM, which means they were quietly rolling at 7:04 AM, when the stage was dark and the room was supposedly empty.

The room was not empty. Tanner was on the stage. Pastor Dave's twenty-four-year-old son. Alone. He had turned on the haze — your haze — and a single front beam — your lighting — and unbuttoned his shirt exactly one button past worship, and he was lip-syncing to a Maverick City track into a gimbal for what is unmistakably "VISION DOWNLOAD" content. There are four takes. On the fourth, he does a slow-motion hair thing. You have all of it. In 4K. On a drive in your hand.

You are now holding the single most powerful object at Execution Church.

## A

You delete all four takes. You do not keep dirt on people, even people who refer to the booth as "an energy" that needs "stewarding." Tanner will never know how close he came. You will know. Sometimes, in meetings, when Tanner says "frequency," you will simply smile, and the smile will be enough, and only you will know why.

## B

You catch Tanner in the hallway and let him know, gently, that the stage cameras are motion-triggered and that whatever they may have captured this morning has been wiped. Tanner's face does several things at once. "Appreciate you, man," he says, in a voice an octave higher than usual. He reschedules his content for a Tuesday. He brings you a coffee on Wednesday. He has never brought anyone a coffee.

## C

You don't delete it. You don't mention it. You drag it into the archive folder labeled, with chilling neutrality, "2024 — MISC." It joins the other things in there. You do not look at the other things in there. You know what's in there. The archive grows. You grow with it.

## D:win

You wait until you're standing next to Tanner, you open the clip, and you AirDrop it to his phone with the caption "great lighting 👍." Then you go back to coiling a cable. Tanner watches it load. Tanner goes the color of haze. Tanner deletes three drafts on the spot. Tanner never again shares a single "initial thought about the tech direction." You have, without a word, established that you are the keeper of everything that happens in this building. Across the lobby, through the glass, Wendell catches your eye and gives you one slow nod — one oracle to another — and you understand that the church has always had two of you, and now you both know it.

## D:lose

You AirDrop the clip. Tanner does not take it as a gentle warning. Tanner panics and sprints ahead of the story, going straight to his father to report that the tech leader "has cameras running on everyone all the time and is sending people disturbing videos." Suddenly your motion-triggered production archive is not a clever asset. It is an HR conversation. It is a policy. It is Pastor Dave asking, very evenly, "What else is on the drive," and you realizing you do not have a comfortable answer.

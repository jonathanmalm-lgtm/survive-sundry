12:30 PM. Second service is in, the door has slowed to a trickle of latecomers, and you have a Sharon situation.

Sharon is a beloved institution. Sharon has greeted at this door for twelve years. Sharon is also, and there is no kind way to chart this, a *long* hugger. Sharon hugs everyone — members, strangers, delivery drivers — and Sharon hugs them for a duration that begins as warmth and crosses, somewhere around the four-second mark, into a hostage situation. Regulars have learned the Sharon hug. They lean in, they accept their fate, they emerge. First-timers do not know. First-timers arrive expecting a handshake and receive, instead, a full-body commitment from a woman they have known for one second.

Right now Sharon has a visibly stiff first-time guy in a six-second embrace, his arms pinned, his eyes scanning the lobby for an authority, and that authority is you. And — this is the part that turns a quirk into a crisis — a teenager near the bulletin table has her phone up, horizontal, recording, with the unmistakable posture of someone who has found content.

## A

You ease over and you coach Sharon with total tenderness, because Sharon's heart is the entire point and you would never break it. "Sharon — you're the warmest person in this building, and I want to protect that. Let's try the two-second side hug for the brand-new folks, save the big ones for our regulars who can handle you." You demonstrate. Sharon practices on you. Sharon nails it. Sharon redeploys with a side hug so gentle and brief it could be in a training video, and the next first-timer gets exactly the right amount of Sharon, which is some Sharon, but not all of Sharon. The teenager puts her phone down. There is no content. The best crises end with no content.

## B

You move Sharon to the coffee bar, which is the perfect Sharon environment: maximum warmth, structurally limited contact, a counter between her and the huggee. "Sharon, I need my best person on coffee — that's the heart of the lobby." Sharon beams; Sharon has been promoted, in her mind, and emotionally she has. You put a calm handshake-and-eye-contact greeter on the door. Sharon spends the rest of the morning telling people their coffee order is "a great choice" and hugging them only when they come around the counter, which is consensual, which is the whole difference. The teenager films nothing. The system worked.

## C

You decide Sharon is Sharon and the people love her and it's fine. It is mostly fine. It is fine until the six-second hug becomes the clip, and the clip gets a caption — "when the church greeter won't let go 💀" — and the clip does numbers, forty-one thousand views by Tuesday, and now the single most-seen piece of media your church will produce this month is a stranger being slowly, lovingly crushed at your door. Sharon doesn't know she's gone viral. You hope she never finds out. The comments are, against all odds, mostly affectionate. That is the only mercy in the whole thing.

## D:win

You make a wild call: you become the buffer. You insert yourself bodily into the greeting zone, intercepting each incoming guest a half-step before Sharon can lock on, converting her full embraces into a tag-team handshake-and-side-pat hand-off so smooth it looks choreographed. Sharon thinks you're *helping greet.* You are running interference at the line of scrimmage. And the teenager's camera — which was aimed at a hostage situation — now captures something else entirely: a guy and a sweet older lady running a flawless two-person welcome like a comedy duo, perfectly timed, weirdly delightful. The caption changes to "this church's greeting team is ELITE 🙌." Twenty-eight thousand views. Pastor Dave sees it and texts you a single thumbs-up. You turned a liability into a brand. Sharon got hugs in. Everybody won. Nobody got crushed. You are very tired.

## D:lose

You go for the buffer move — wedging yourself between Sharon and the guests — but the timing is off, and instead of a smooth hand-off it looks, on camera, like a man repeatedly diving between an affectionate grandmother and innocent strangers, body-checking a guest, and at one point briefly group-hugging both Sharon and a startled first-timer in a confused three-person clinch you cannot explain. *That's* the clip. "idk what's happening at this church but I can't look away 💀," fifty-three thousand views, your face fully visible, lunging. Sharon is hurt — "were you trying to keep people from hugging me?" — which is exactly what you were doing, and now you have to either lie to Sharon or admit you've been managing her hugs for twelve years. Pastor Dave watches the clip in his office. Pastor Dave watches it twice. Pastor Dave does not text a thumbs-up.

## Ending 1

Pastor Dave is at his desk. Not on his phone. There's a banker's box on the corner of it, and on top of the box is your lanyard, your name-tag printer, and the good label maker you brought from home and never told anyone you brought from home. He lets the silence do the early work. "We love you. We believe God has a plan for your life." A pause. "We've been talking with a few people about the door." The meeting takes eleven minutes. On the way out, Brenda from admin hands you a card. Someone drew a little welcome mat on it. Someone wrote "God has a plan!" You sit in your car in the lot you greeted in this morning. You look at the card. Wendell, finishing his rounds, gives you one slow, weighted thumbs-up through the windshield. He is not being cruel. It is the only goodbye Wendell knows.

Your first listing is Ron's house. Ron has eleven years of opinions about the front door, and now they are professionally relevant.

## Ending 2

Pastor Dave slides a piece of paper across the desk. "First Impressions Scheduling and Compliance Coordinator." *Compliance.* The word sits on the desk like a wet bulletin. "We think your attention to systems is genuinely a gift," he says. "You're going to be great at identifying gaps." You drive home with the word *compliance* riding shotgun.

You will spend the next three years building greeter rotations in Planning Center for greeters who do not read Planning Center, and maintaining a master lamination schedule that Wendell will quietly correct. You never gave Wendell your work email. Wendell cc's himself on the schedule anyway. Wendell always knows.

## Ending 3

The meeting starts normally. Then the door opens, and it's Tanner — 24, back from his fifth year at a two-year Bible college, leather journal under his arm with "VISION DOWNLOAD" embossed on the cover in a font he chose himself. He came back from Bethel with a vocabulary that is mostly the word "activation." He sits down next to you. "I've had some initial thoughts about the *energy* of the front door," he says. "God's been speaking to me about thresholds. About frequency." You look at Pastor Dave. Pastor Dave is already looking at his phone.

Tanner's first directive is to replace the connection cards with a QR code "to lower the friction." His second directive is to greet people "from a place of overflow, not obligation." His third is to bring in fog. *More* fog. For the lobby. On purpose.

## Ending 4

You're in the parking lot, four minutes before the meeting, when your phone rings. Unknown number, local area code. "This is Jason — I'm a pastor over at Redemption City Church. We've been watching how your team handles a Sunday morning, and honestly, the *guest experience* is the thing nobody gets right and you get right. We'd love to talk." Of course you've heard of Redemption City. The megachurch down the street, the one with a real budget, valet parking, and a lobby that smells, somehow, like a nicer life.

You walk into the meeting with Pastor Dave anyway. From your side, it's autopilot; you hear about every third word, because the rest of you is already standing in that other, better lobby. Wendell, out in the lot, somehow already knows about the call. Wendell always knows. He gives you a thumbs-up that is, for once, a little sad. You take the meeting. You start at Redemption City in two months.

## Ending 5

He's smiling when you walk in — the real one. "I've been doing this for twenty-two years," he says. "Most people manage a Sunday. A few people set the temperature of the whole building before anyone hears a word from the stage. That's you. That's the door." He slides a notepad across the desk. Specific bullet points: the family you walked in, the lost boy you kept calm, the way the room *felt* warm to people who couldn't have told you why. He noticed all of it. "Well done. Good and faithful servant." He flips the notepad. "Now — let's plan next Sunday."

Wendell filed a laminated report on the morning. It was positive. He brought you a copy. Pastor Dave already had a copy. Of course he did.

## Ending 6

He walks you through a color-coded recap of the month. The wins are green. The growth areas are yellow. There is one red, and you know which one it is before his pen gets there. He circles it once. "The team trusts you. The congregation feels it on Sunday mornings — they really do. And you actually know where the gaps are, which is rarer than you think." He shakes his head slowly, not in disappointment, in the way of a man recalibrating. "We'll do better next time."

The recap lives on your desk for three weeks. You look at the red circle every morning. Tom's chairs are not on it. This surprises you, a little, every single day.

## Ending 7

The meeting goes well. Suspiciously well. He slides an envelope across the desk. The salary adjustment works out to roughly $83 a month. "It's not huge. But it's meaningful. It's a symbol." On the way home you stop at the store and stand in the pasta aisle for a long time. You pick up the store-brand ramen. You put it back. You pick up the Maruchan. You hold it a moment, realizing you can afford it now, this exact thing, today.

You frame the letter. It hangs a little crooked on the wall by the door. You leave it crooked. It feels right by the door.

## Ending 8

He presents the new title, and it's genuinely better: Director of Guest Experience. Then he presents the responsibilities. The list is long. "—the full first-impressions team, plus hospitality, plus parking" — *parking,* which means Wendell, which means you now technically oversee a man who oversees you — "plus you'll help launch the guest experience at the second campus in the fall, and the budget holds where it is for now, we'll revisit in—" he checks something "—eighteen to twenty-four months." He closes the folder, beaming. "You earned this." You shake his hand. You mean it. You get to your car. You open your calendar. You look at it for a long time. You put the phone face-down on the seat. You sit there a minute. Then you drive home, the new title already filling like a lobby.

## Wrong Name Win

At one point in the meeting Pastor Dave pauses. "Our guest this morning — he had a wonderful time. Said the welcome was the best he's experienced anywhere." You nod, warmly, professionally, and you do not say his name, because you still do not know his name. He left as Gary. He arrived as a stranger and departed as Gary and somewhere in between four greeters, two coffee pourers, a child, and the senior pastor all called him Gary and not one of them cracked, and the man answered to it every time like he was born to it. You will never learn who he actually was. He is out there right now, somewhere, a regional somebody, telling people about the lovely church where everyone knew his name. They did not know his name. They knew *a* name. You have made a quiet, lasting peace with this. Out in the lot, Wendell — who knew the real name all along, who has it written down somewhere, laminated — catches your eye and gives you one slow nod. Two men who understand that a name withheld is sometimes the gentlest gift of all.

## Wrong Name Lose

"One more thing," Pastor Dave says, and his voice does the thing where it gets calmer as the subject gets worse. "Our guest this morning." A pause. "That was the new regional overseer. His signature is on the building loan. The actual loan, for the actual building we are sitting in." Another pause. "His name is Donald." He looks at you. "Donald." He lets it land. "Don*ald.*" Three times, clearly, slowly, while looking directly at you, the way you say a name to someone who has demonstrated they cannot be trusted with names. "He mentioned that the entire greeting team called him Gary all morning. Every single one. With great confidence." Pastor Dave folds his hands. "He thought it was — and I'm quoting — 'a bold choice.'" The silence goes on a while. "Let's chat this week," Pastor Dave says, which you now understand is the sound the building makes when it remembers whose signature holds it up.

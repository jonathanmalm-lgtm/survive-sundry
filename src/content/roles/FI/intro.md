Your alarm goes off at 6:14 AM. You set it to 6:14 because 6:15 felt aspirational and you have never once been a 6:15 person. Someone already made the coffee. This is a sign, and you read signs for a living.

Today you will greet somewhere between four hundred and six hundred people, and every single one of them will decide how they feel about Execution Church in the thirty seconds before they reach the auditorium doors. Thirty seconds. You have a spreadsheet about those thirty seconds. You have never shown anyone the spreadsheet.

You take this seriously. You take it so seriously that the sentence "it's a door, {first name}" has been said to you, out loud, by at least three different staff members, in a tone they thought was gentle. It was not gentle. A door is the first thing four hundred people touch. A door is a theology.

You are short two greeters today. You found out at 9:40 last night, in the form of two texts forty minutes apart, both of which used the phrase "so sorry" and neither of which used the word why. You filled the first slot by 10:15. The second slot is still open, and you have been lying in the dark assembling a mental list of everyone who owes you a favor.

Welcome to Execution Church. Today is Sunday. The mission statement says "Make disciples or else…" and the door is where the "or else" begins.

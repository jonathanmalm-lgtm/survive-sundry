9:00 AM. The all-team huddle, the one moment all morning the whole building is in one room before it scatters. The worship leader is here looking like she's already led two services. The tech guy is in all black, blinking at the daylight like a mole. Pastor Dave stands at the back with his coffee, present and saying nothing, which is its own kind of pressure.

Pastor Dave produces the $5 Chick-fil-A gift card. This was decided at staff meeting on Tuesday — the card goes to "the volunteer closest to burnout," which is a phrase the staff says with great tenderness and zero self-awareness. He calls Terry's name. Terry has a four-week-old at home, has not slept since Lent, and is here, in the lobby, at 9 AM, in a collared shirt, holding it together by what can only be the literal grace of God. Terry accepts the five-dollar card with the wet eyes of a man who would very much like a nap and is going to get a chicken sandwich instead. It is, you note, exactly enough for one sandwich and not the fries.

Then Greg is introduced. Former worship pastor at a well-known church that recently came apart at the seams over what the staff calls "a season." He's here to "observe and encourage," and today, specifically, he's "experiencing the guest journey" — which means Greg is going to walk your lobby as a customer and grade it. Greg has a very good jacket. Greg scans your welcome table like a man who has owned a nicer one.

And now it's your turn to send your short-handed people out the door, and you can feel the verse forming in your mouth, the one everyone reaches for, the one that isn't about this at all.

## A

You skip the inspirational theology entirely. You go around the half-circle and you name them — "Becca, you've got the south door; Doug, you're on coffee, *all* the coffee; Terry, take the easy post by the kids' wing, you've earned a slow morning." You tell them the plain truth: you're two greeters down, it's going to be a workout, and you'd rather be short-staffed with this room than fully staffed with anyone else. Nobody cries except Terry, but Terry is crying about everything today, so it doesn't count. They go to their posts standing a little taller.

## B

You give a forty-five-second talk with no fat on it. Positions, partners, one rule: "Nobody walks in alone and nobody walks out alone. If you see a person standing still, you move." You assign your two strongest greeters to float the gaps. Greg, watching from the side, writes something in a small leather notebook, and for once you don't care what it is, because the huddle was clean and your people know exactly where to stand. You'd run through a wall for these eleven people. Three of them, frankly, would run through a wall for a maple bar, but the spirit is there.

## C

You keep it airy. "Big morning, lots of new faces, let's love people well, let's have a great one!" You clap once, which is the universal sign for *please disperse before there are questions.* It works. Nobody asks about the two missing greeters. Nobody asks who's covering the gym entrance. They will find out who's covering the gym entrance the way you find out about a step in the dark, and the answer will be nobody, and the discovery will be made by a family of five.

## D:win

You go for the verse, full voice. "Listen — God won't give you more than you can handle. First Corinthians 10:13." Your people nod, fortified by a sentiment that has nothing to do with their situation. Then, because you are a professional, you make it a graphic — cream background, serif font, a little watercolor sunrise — and you drop it in the volunteer group chat under the words "WE GOT THIS." It rips. Fourteen heart-reactions. Becca makes it her phone wallpaper. Nobody on your team knows the verse is about resisting temptation and not about staffing, and crucially, neither does anyone who can fire you. Morale is, against all exegetical odds, extremely high.

## D:lose

You deliver the verse with conviction — "God won't give you more than you can handle, First Corinthians 10:13" — and you make the watercolor-sunrise graphic, and you post it to the group chat. What you forget is that Greg is *on* the volunteer group chat now, "just to observe the culture," and Greg went to actual seminary. Greg replies to the graphic, in front of your whole team, with: "Love the heart on this 🙏 — fun fact, that verse is actually about temptation, not workload! Common one." A little exegetical knife, wrapped in a praying-hands emoji. Three volunteers Google it. Pastor Dave, who is also on the chat, reacts to Greg's message with a thumbs-up. Not yours. Greg's.

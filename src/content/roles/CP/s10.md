12:40 PM. Office. Door closed. You should be eating the granola bar in your drawer. Instead your phone has buzzed with a Google Alert, and you already know the search it's attached to, because you set it up, in the dark, fourteen months ago: **Redemption City Church.**

The megachurch down the street. Eleven thousand a weekend to your fourteen hundred. A parking lot you could land a plane on. A worship band with a guy who's toured. Tom from ushers once said their lobby "has a coffee bar with *latte art*" and you thought about it for three days.

You have a spreadsheet. Nobody knows about the spreadsheet. Tabs: Attendance, Giving (estimated), Building Sq Ft, Guest Artists, Parking Acreage. You update it most Sundays. You have prayed about the spreadsheet. You have not deleted the spreadsheet. The alert is a new sermon series of theirs — gorgeous art, a one-word title, a teaser reel with eleven thousand views — and you can feel the old familiar acid climbing, the coveting you confess every Sunday around minute eleven and commit again by minute twelve.

You happen to have a number for one of their staffers. From a conference. You could just... ask.

## A

You close the alert. You close the spreadsheet. You open the prayer you keep bookmarked — the one about the man who's content in plenty and in want — and you actually pray it, slowly, instead of skimming it like a contract. The acid recedes. Eleven thousand is their assignment. Fourteen hundred is yours. You eat the granola bar. It's the one with chocolate chips, which you'd been saving, and saving it turns out to have been the whole point.

## B

You text their campus pastor — you got his number at a conference — but you text the true thing, with no hook in it: "Saw your new series art. It's beautiful. Hope your team's surviving the morning. Pulling for you over here." Three dots. "Man, thank you. Brutal morning actually — lost two volunteers and a projector. Means a lot. Pulling for you too." And just like that the megachurch down the street is fourteen people drowning in the same Sunday you are, and the acid turns to something almost like fellowship. You eat the granola bar. You don't open the spreadsheet. You don't even remember it's there.

## C

You don't text anyone. You just open the spreadsheet and enter today's numbers — fourteen hundred and twelve, give or take the kids' wing — into the cell next to their eleven thousand, and you sit in the gap. You don't pray. You don't eat. You just look at the two numbers until the granola bar goes soft in your hand. Nobody sees you do this. That's the worst part and the only mercy of it. You'll do it again next Sunday. The spreadsheet is the only place you let yourself want things, and it is a terrible place to keep wanting things, and you keep them there anyway.

## D:win

You text their staffer with the voice — collaborative, collegial, generous. "Hey! Would love to compare notes pastor-to-pastor sometime — always think we're better when we're rooting for each other. Out of curiosity, what are you guys running these days, and roughly what's a normal week look like for giving? Trying to benchmark." The friendliness is the lockpick. It works. The numbers come back — real ones, the ones that aren't in the annual report — and you enter them into the spreadsheet with a hand that is not entirely steady. For about ninety seconds you feel superior. Then you feel the exact shape of the ninety seconds, and the hole in your chest, which is the only thing about you that updates faster than the spreadsheet. Nobody will ever know. You will always know.

## D:lose

You text their staffer the collaborative-collegial fishing line — "pastor-to-pastor," "benchmark," "rooting for each other" — and ask, warmly, for their real attendance and giving numbers. Their staffer reads it. Their staffer has gotten this exact text from envious small-church pastors before and can smell a benchmark from a mile out. He does not reply to you. He screenshots it, adds a little "lol is this guy serious," and forwards it to *his* pastor — who, it turns out, knows Pastor Dave, because of course he does, because the megachurch pastors all golf. By Tuesday Pastor Dave has the screenshot. "Got a funny text forwarded to me," he says, not laughing. "Were you trying to get Redemption City's numbers?" There is no vague-but-impressive word for the silence that follows. The thing you coveted now has a screenshot of you coveting it.

You're in the lobby during the message — circulating, available, present for the people who aren't in the room. Ron finds you. Ron always finds you.

Ron has been here eleven years. Ron has feedback today, prepared, for you specifically. The sermon is good, he says — and he opens there, which is how you know there's a *but* loaded — but the series has run long, and congregational engagement was "visibly declining by week four." Ron has been tracking engagement. Nobody asked Ron to track engagement. Ron tracked it anyway, in a notebook, with dates.

Then Ron reminds you, not unkindly, what he tithes each month. It is, to the dollar, your entire salary. From one household. Ron wants you to hold that fact while he talks. Ron is not threatening you. Ron is providing context. The distinction matters enormously to Ron and costs you the same either way.

## A

You give Ron the full nineteen minutes, right there against the lobby wall. Some of what Ron says is accurate. Some is not. You receive both with the same face. At the end Ron says, "I appreciate you taking the time." "I appreciate you caring," you say, and you both mean it more than the words. You go home and pull the engagement data yourself. Ron was not wrong about week four. Ron is annoyingly not wrong about week four.

## B

You give Ron a container. "Here's what I'm hearing —" and you say it back, cleanly, so he knows it landed. He says his whole piece in four minutes because you made it safe to be brief. "Monday," he confirms. Monday's meeting runs sixteen minutes and produces two things: a genuinely useful read on the series arc, and a mutual respect neither of you would say out loud but both of you walk out holding. Ron tithes the same either way. You just made the relationship worth the salary.

## C

Ron notices. Of course Ron notices — Ron tracks engagement for fun. He finishes his thought knowing you weren't fully receiving it. "I appreciate your time," he says, in a way that carries everything he isn't saying. His follow-up arrives Wednesday by email, single-spaced, and he CCs Pastor Dave. Nobody asked Ron to CC Pastor Dave. Ron felt it was appropriate, and Ron's sense of what is appropriate is now your Wednesday afternoon.

## D:win

"Ron," you say, like the thought is arriving fresh and not from a place of calculated desperation, "has anyone ever talked to you about a role? Director of Congregational Care. You see things the rest of us miss." Ron stops. Ron has not considered this. You watch the feedback in him quietly reroute into flattery. "I'd want to do it right," Ron says, already softer. It works. You have neutralized your loudest critic by handing him a lanyard. It is a deeply cynical maneuver and you'll answer for it later, but for now Ron schedules Monday to "discuss the role," and the series critique evaporates like haze.

## D:lose

"Has anyone ever talked to you about a role?" you say, reaching for the title. Ron looks at you for a long, level moment. "Is this because of what I said about the series?" "No," you say, too fast. "It's about your investment in this church." Ron hears the word *investment* and his whole face changes, because Ron knows exactly what his investment is, down to the dollar, and he has just watched you reach for his wallet wearing a clergy collar. "I'd like to think about it," Ron says. Ron calls the board chair Monday. The board chair calls you Monday afternoon. "Did you offer Ron a staff title in the lobby — the same morning you told him the series was too long?" The line goes quiet. You go quiet. "Let's talk Wednesday."

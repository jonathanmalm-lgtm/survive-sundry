8:00 AM. You walk past the stage and Brody is unraveling in the wings. The in-ears aren't getting the click track — the tech leader is mid-surgery on a subwoofer that died Thursday and is somehow still dying — and the fourth song has a programmed guitar part that nobody can hear, which means Brody is about to lead a song he cannot follow.

He turns to you with the eyes of a man who has not eaten and has watched the click track abandon him in real time. "I can't do the fourth one without the click. I can't. It's going to be a train wreck." Behind him, two of the seven band members aren't here yet, and one texted "omw" in a way that everyone present understood to mean "leaving now."

This is a forty-five-second window. He needs a decision more than he needs a conversation. You have both in you. You only have time to give one.

## A

You put a hand on the back of his neck and walk him three steps from the stage. "Talk." He vents for ninety seconds — the click, the band, the subwoofer, his blood sugar. You let all of it land. Then: "You've led this room without a click forty times. Today's forty-one. Drop the fourth song if you want it gone, keep it if you don't. Either way I've got you." He nods. The panic drains out of his shoulders. He keeps the song. It's fine. He needed to hand you the fear, not the decision.

## B

"Three songs, not four. I'm making that call, it's mine, if anyone asks it was me." Brody blinks, then sags with relief — you just took the thing he was afraid of and put it in your own pocket. He doesn't have to be the guy who cut a song. You are. The set is tight and clean. The room never knows there was a fourth song. Leadership is sometimes just volunteering to be the reason.

## C

"It'll be fine!" you say, with a smile, already walking. Brody watches you go and understands that he is alone with this. He leads all four songs. The fourth is, in fact, a train wreck — the band scatters off the missing click like startled pigeons. From the booth it's a slow-motion collision. Brody recovers it by the bridge, but he spends the rest of the morning knowing you left, and you spend the rest of the morning knowing it too.

## D:win

"You know," you say slowly, dropping into the voice, "I've been feeling a check in my spirit about that fourth song all morning. I think we're supposed to let it go." Brody's whole face changes. You have just converted "the in-ears are broken" into "God said." It is theologically reckless and it works instantly — you cannot argue with a check in the spirit, that's the entire point of a check in the spirit. The song gets cut. The set is great. Only you know the Holy Spirit had nothing to do with it and the subwoofer had everything.

## D:lose

"I've been feeling a check in my spirit about the fourth song," you say. Brody nods solemnly and cuts it. Unfortunately the fourth song was the one Pastor Dave specifically requested for the baby dedication, and Pastor Dave finds you at turnover to ask, gently, where his song went. "I felt a check in my spirit," you say, and hear how it sounds the instant it leaves your mouth. Pastor Dave looks at you the way you look at people who blame the Holy Spirit for their logistics. "Did you," he says. It is not a question.

You arrive at 7:05 AM. Wendell is already in the lot, setting out the cones in the order only Wendell knows. Wendell gives you a thumbs up. You give him a thumbs up. This is the entire relationship and it is the healthiest one you have at this church.

You are eleven steps from the door when Dennis steps out from between two parked Highlanders. Dennis is a congregant. Dennis is holding a gas-station coffee and the face of a man who has rehearsed an opening line for two weeks and has decided that this lot, this morning, is where it gets said.

Dennis's marriage is struggling. He tells you this quietly, between the bumpers, at 7:05 on a Sunday. "I don't know if this is the time," he says. It is not the time. He has already started. He is also not wrong that he needs to say it to someone.

You have a full Sunday in your head. You also have Dennis. The list in the back of your mind quietly adds Dennis's wife, then rebukes itself.

## A

You are twenty-two minutes late to the huddle prep. The worship leader covers. The morning absorbs it, the way it absorbs everything. Dennis leaves the lot lighter than he arrived. You schedule a real appointment for Monday, and the Monday appointment becomes three more, and the three more go somewhere you'll never fully see, because ministry doesn't have a final slide. It just keeps going. You walk in at 7:27 with your coffee cold and your spirit oddly warm.

## B

Two minutes. You let him say the worst sentence out loud, then you say it back to him so he knows it landed. You hand him a real time — "Monday, 8 AM, my office, I'll have coffee." Dennis exhales like a man who came to surrender something and got to keep his dignity. He goes inside. He sits closer to the front than he meant to. You walk in at 7:09, on time, carrying one more name.

## C

Dennis looks at you. "Right. Okay." He goes inside. He sits in the second-to-last row, the row people choose when they aren't sure they want to be seen. He sings the songs. He shakes your hand at the door and does not meet your eye. He does not come back next Sunday. You will think about Dennis on Wednesday, briefly, between two other things.

## D:win

You set a hand on his shoulder and lower your voice into the register you use for moments like this. "Dennis," you say, "I sense the Lord is doing something in you in this season — a refining. A breaking that's actually a building." It means nothing. It means everything. It is calibrated to land on any man standing between two Highlanders at 7 AM with a bad coffee.

Dennis's eyes fill. "You have no idea how much I needed to hear that," he says. You do, actually. That's the whole skill. He drives off lighter, you're inside by 7:08, and you spend the first worship song quietly asking God to forgive you for being good at this.

## D:lose

You set a hand on his shoulder and reach for vague, but your mouth, in a hurry, reaches for specific. "I sense God is restoring what was lost — that the thing with your job is about to turn a corner." Dennis blinks. "I didn't say anything about my job." "Spiritually," you say. "The spiritual job."

It does not turn a corner. Dennis is laid off Thursday. By Sunday he has told four people the pastor said God told him his job was safe. The story grows a verb each time it's retold. By the elders' meeting it is "the pastor prophesied over him in the parking lot." Pastor Dave asks you about it with a patience that is its own kind of correction.

Twenty minutes after second service. Everyone's standing. Nobody's eaten since the huddle donuts four hours ago. This is not a meeting with food. This is the meeting where you find out what people actually thought, and you run it.

You know more about this Sunday than anyone in the room. You were in the lot with Dennis at 7:05. You were in the wings when Brody unraveled. You held a screaming child named Maverick Cannon. You were cornered by Ron and by row nine. You alone know about the spreadsheet. You carry all of it.

Then Tom — head usher, the insecure one, the one who measures the chairs — opens with, "I just want to be honest, I felt like the worship transitions had some *moments* today." He says it with the gentle certainty of a man sure he's helping. What Tom means is that Brody cut a song. What Tom is doing is teeing up a miss and rolling it toward a teammate who is standing right there. Pastor Dave is in the room. Pastor Dave is not on his phone. Pastor Dave is watching you decide what kind of leader runs this meeting.

## A

You go first and you go to the misses, and you put your own at the top — the lobby gap, the song you let get cut, the place you pushed Brody when he needed cover. By the time you've named three of your own, the room has relaxed into honesty instead of defense, and Tom's "moments" comment has nowhere dangerous to go because the pastor already volunteered to be the first one under the bus. Brody, unthrown, names two real fixes. Pastor Dave doesn't write anything. He just watches a room tell the truth safely, which is the rarest thing he sees all week.

## B

Clean and fair. Wins first, with names. Misses second, with fixes attached and the absent protected — "Let's not relitigate the projector, the volunteer who ran it isn't here to speak for himself." When Tom rolls his "moments" toward Brody, you catch it midair: "The cut song was my call, Tom. Took it in the wings. Talk to me." Tom's miss has nowhere to land. The room sees a meeting that's safe to be honest in. That safety is the only thing that makes a Hits & Misses worth having.

## C

You keep it shallow. Thanks all around, a couple of safe wins, no teeth. You adjourn before anything real can surface, including Tom's thing, including yours. Everyone goes home unscarred and unhelped. Pastor Dave gives a small nod that is not approval and not disapproval — it is the nod of a man noting that the meeting that exists to find the truth was, today, gently steered around it. You'll feel that nod Tuesday.

## D:win

You take Tom's "moments" and you run with it, calm as still water, and you reroute the whole morning. "You know, I think a lot of today came down to the flow," you say, with the unhurried warmth of a man who is definitely not scapegoating. "And honestly some of it traces back to the projector — Doug had a rough go with it, and he's already headed home, so let's give him grace and just tighten the handoff next week." Doug is not here. Doug cannot defend the projector. "The flow" cannot defend itself either, because "the flow" is not a person. The room, relieved to have somewhere bloodless to put the blame, nods and moves on. It works. Nobody's hurt in the room because you aimed it at a man who already left and a noun that can't feel anything. On the drive home you'll know exactly what you did, and you'll know it was the pastoral equivalent of a sleight of hand, and you'll know you're good at it, which is the part that bothers you.

## D:lose

You reroute the morning onto "the flow" and onto Doug, who went home — calm, warm, pastoral, the whole instrument. But Pastor Dave was at first service. Pastor Dave watched the projector run perfectly at first service. Pastor Dave knows Doug left early *because* his part went fine. And so when you lay the morning at the feet of "the flow" and an absent volunteer, Pastor Dave does not argue. Pastor Dave just lets the silence sit there, in front of everyone, and looks at you, and the silence does the thing you've watched silence do in his hands a hundred times. The room slowly understands that the pastor just blamed a guy who isn't here. Brody understands he was almost the guy. Tom, who started this, suddenly finds his shoes very interesting. "Anything else?" Pastor Dave asks, mildly, into your meeting. There is not. "Same time next week." Four seconds. Pastor Dave moves first.

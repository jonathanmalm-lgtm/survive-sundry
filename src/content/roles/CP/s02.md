You walk into the staff area and feel it before you see it: the exact barometric pressure of two people who exchanged something at 11 PM that did not get resolved, and who are now in the same room pretending it did.

It's Brody, your worship leader, and Brenda from admin. They were both here for the late stage reset last night. Something happened — not a blowup, something smaller, the kind of small thing that becomes a large thing if nobody touches it. Neither of them has touched it, because it's Sunday and there is too much else.

They are both doing their jobs. They are doing them about fifteen percent louder than usual. Brody is coiling a cable like it owes him money. Brenda is stapling bulletins with conviction. You notice all of it. Noticing is the job. The list in the back of your head adds two names and you tell it to sit down.

## A

Three minutes. You close the door. "Something happened last night, and I need both of you all the way here today." You don't know what happened. You don't ask. "Whatever it is, it waits until Monday. Can you give me today?" They both say yes, and both yeses are real. Monday it comes out, and Monday it turns out to be smaller than Saturday night dressed it up to be. It usually is.

## B

You find Brody first. "I'm fine," he says, which is what people say. You wait. "We'll talk Monday." "Yes, you will," you say. You find Brenda. "We'll handle it Monday." "Good." They both do their jobs well. The pressure doesn't vanish. It drops to a level a room can hold.

## C

The pressure rides along all morning. By first service it has hardened into professionalism. By second service it's genuinely fine. Monday slides to Tuesday slides to Thursday, and by Thursday they've handled it themselves without you. You learn it's resolved when Brenda sends Brody a calendar invite for next Saturday's reset and he accepts it within the minute. Sometimes the best pastoral care you offer is staying out of it.

## D:win

You build it into the message — a "purely hypothetical" story about "two staff members who let a small thing harden overnight." You preach it warmly, gently, to four hundred people, while making eye contact with the carpet. Brody and Brenda are both in the room. Both of them know. Neither looks at the other. Both look at you with the specific stillness of people who have just been addressed by name without their names.

It works. They find each other in the lobby and resolve it in ninety seconds. It also costs you something you can't quite invoice. You used the pulpit as a tool and the tool, this once, did the job.

## D:lose

You preach the "two hypothetical staff members." Unfortunately, the hypothetical is detailed — a late night, a stage reset, a cable, a stapler. Brody and Brenda go pale in unison. So do four other staff members, each of whom is now privately certain the sermon was about them. Three of them find you in the lobby to ask if they're okay with you. The story that travels is not "the pastor preached on unity." The story that travels is "the pastor subtweeted his own staff from the stage." Pastor Dave heard the sermon. Pastor Dave knows what a stapler is. "Let's chat this week," he says.
